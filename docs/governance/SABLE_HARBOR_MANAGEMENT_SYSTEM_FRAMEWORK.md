# SABLE HARBOR MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (SHMS) — DEVELOPMENT FRAMEWORK

**Document ID:** `SH-GOV-SHMS-000` | **Version:** 0.0.1 | **Date:** September 3, 2026 | **State:** NEXT PRIORITY / NOT YET CANON
**Owner:** Office of the CEO | **Tracking:** GitHub issue #87

## Purpose

This file exists to make the next development thread explicit. It is **not** a completed management system and must not be cited as if its undeveloped sections were doctrine.

SHMS is intended to be a short doctrine and curated body of management practice for how Sable Harbor uses judgment, authority, information, capital, learning, and institutional capability. It is not an information system, software platform, audit framework, process office, or universal checklist.

J2 is the institutional information/judgment/orientation capability. SHMS is management doctrine for using those capabilities well.

## Locked development boundaries

- CEO is the principle owner.
- Stewardship remains tiny and non-enforcing.
- SHMS must not become an adoption scorecard, certification program, process bureaucracy, shadow management layer, or corporate command function.
- New bureaucracy carries the burden of proof.
- The management system must preserve Sable Harbor's autonomy, continuity, information-symmetry, capital-allocation, anti-meeting, and anti-surveillance principles.
- Existing canon controls; SHMS may not silently overwrite it.

## Required archaeology pass

Before drafting polished SHMS doctrine:

1. inventory actual accepted principles across corporate canon, governance, J2, ESS, People & Culture, Technology Services, portfolio/authority/capital doctrine, and business-line lore;
2. cite each principle to its source;
3. classify it as LOCKED, strongly implied, PROVISIONAL, or OPEN;
4. identify contradictions without silently reconciling them;
5. identify repeated practices that deserve a named management discipline;
6. identify practices that are specific to one function/business and must **not** become universal;
7. test every proposed universal discipline against bureaucracy cost and local-autonomy impact.

## Candidate development domains — QUESTIONS, NOT CANON

These headings are the extraction map for the archaeology pass. They do not establish methods yet.

- Judgment and decision quality
- Authority and delegation
- Information symmetry and dissent
- Capital allocation
- Planning and performance
- Talent, continuity, and succession
- Problem solving and issue rooms
- Institutional learning and AAR
- Professional practice stewardship
- Business autonomy and enterprise boundaries
- Experimentation, failure, and controlled bets
- Bureaucracy pruning and meeting discipline
- Management formation

## Required artifact set

When SHMS is developed, publish it under the three-representation rule:

1. canonical Markdown;
2. controlled corporate publication using the approved Sable Harbor document system;
3. structured representation/database entry.

The first substantive SHMS deliverable should be the **source-linked principle inventory**, not a glossy handbook.

## Explicitly out of scope for the first SHMS pass

- detailed Common Controls Framework mechanics;
- Alexandria runtime/entitlements implementation;
- detailed audit procedure design;
- a generic leadership competency matrix;
- invented maturity models;
- failing-business intervention doctrine unless separately reopened;
- organic new-business/incubation doctrine unless separately reopened.

## Completion test for the archaeology phase

The archaeology phase is complete only when a reviewer can answer: **Which Sable Harbor decisions gave rise to each proposed SHMS principle, and what did we deliberately choose not to universalize?**
