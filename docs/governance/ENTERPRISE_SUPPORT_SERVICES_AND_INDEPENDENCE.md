# ENTERPRISE SUPPORT SERVICES AND INDEPENDENCE DOCTRINE

**Document ID:** `SH-GOV-ESS-001` | **Version:** 1.0.0 | **Effective:** September 3, 2026 | **State:** LOCKED
**Owner:** Enterprise Support Services | **Related:** OGC, Compliance, Risk, Internal Audit, Board Audit & Compliance Committee, J2

## Enterprise Support Services

**Enterprise Support Services (ESS)** is the administrative umbrella for Sable Harbor's deliberately low-authority governance and support cluster. The name is intentionally modest. ESS is not an enterprise command center, risk czardom, or shadow management layer.

ESS encompasses:

- the Office of the General Counsel;
- Legal;
- Compliance;
- a modest enterprise risk-coordination function led by the Chief of Risk or equivalent role; and
- Internal Audit for administrative association only.

ESS is an administrative and physical grouping, **not a single substantive reporting chain**. Its existence may never be used to collapse the independence of its constituent functions.

## Reporting lines

The General Counsel reports to the Chief Executive Officer and has direct access to the Board when professional duties require it. Legal and Compliance sit under the General Counsel. The enterprise risk-coordination function is deliberately modest and sits within ESS; it coordinates enterprise risk information and escalation but does not own risks that properly belong to operating management.

Internal Audit is structurally separate from the General Counsel, Compliance, Risk, and J2. Internal Audit reports **functionally to the Board Audit & Compliance Committee**. Its administrative relationship is to the Chief Executive Officer for matters such as enterprise administration, logistics, and employment support that do not impair audit independence.

Compliance has an unfiltered escalation right to the Audit & Compliance Committee where necessary. The General Counsel's board access and Internal Audit's functional reporting are institutional protections, not personal favors.

## Independence doctrine

Sable Harbor maintains separate challenge mechanisms because combining them would weaken all of them.

1. **Management owns the business and its risks.** Operating leaders own controls, remediation, performance, and risk within delegated authority.
2. **Legal and Compliance advise and challenge.** They do not acquire operating command merely because a matter carries legal or compliance exposure.
3. **Risk coordinates; it does not steer.** The risk function aggregates, challenges, connects, and escalates but does not become the enterprise's decision-maker.
4. **Internal Audit independently evaluates.** It tests controls, representations, governance, and other auditable matters without owning the activities it audits.
5. **J2 remains separate.** J2 establishes Contact, forms Judgment, and provides Orientation. It is not Internal Audit, Compliance, Legal, or Risk and cannot use privileged access to become a policing organization.

Internal Audit may audit J2 processes where within scope. J2 may surface facts or uncertainty relevant to Internal Audit. Neither supervises the other, and neither may rely on institutional proximity as a substitute for evidence or independent judgment.

No ESS leader may suppress, pre-clear, edit for convenience, or condition an Internal Audit communication to the Audit & Compliance Committee. Internal Audit controls its audit scope, methods, findings, and communications subject to its charter and committee oversight.

## Physical design

Legal, Compliance, and Risk may share a building and may be colocated for administrative efficiency. Internal Audit should be physically distinct even when housed within the same broader headquarters complex—preferably a separate floor, secured suite, or comparable boundary that reinforces independence without creating isolation.

J2 is physically separate from ESS. Future Sacramento headquarters floor plans should preserve these boundaries explicitly.

## Anti-empire rule

ESS exists to support the enterprise, not to steer it. It may not convert legal review, compliance monitoring, risk coordination, or assurance into generalized approval rights over ordinary business decisions unless a specific law, board-reserved matter, policy, or delegated authority expressly requires such approval.

The governing principle is: **challenge must be strong; authority must remain explicit.**
