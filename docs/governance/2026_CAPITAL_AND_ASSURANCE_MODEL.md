## Capital authority

Sable Harbor uses tiered delegated authority rather than concentrating every commitment with the board or allowing business lines unrestricted autonomy.

Authority escalates according to both **economic magnitude** and **risk character**. A low-dollar action may therefore require escalation when it creates unusual legal, safety, security, environmental, nuclear/federal, data-rights, related-party, reputational, or irreversible operating exposure.

The operating chain is:

1. operating/process management within approved budgets and delegated limits;
2. business-line or operating-company leadership for larger commitments and exceptions;
3. enterprise finance/executive approval for material commitments, unbudgeted expenditures, financing implications, and cross-company consequences;
4. Finance & Investment Committee review/recommendation where appropriate;
5. full-board approval for reserved matters.

Exact dollar thresholds are implementation details and should be calibrated to Sable Harbor's eventual scale rather than invented as audit artifacts.

### Reserved-matter classes

The board retains authority over matters such as transformative acquisitions/dispositions, material debt/equity financing, extraordinary capital programs, changes to corporate control, material related-party transactions, and other matters designated in governance documents.

### Separation of authorities

- Atlas Meridian may provide analysis supporting a capital decision but cannot approve that decision.
- A field/site operating owner may stop, constrain, or place an operation in a conservative state when immediate operating or safety conditions warrant; this does not confer general capital authority.
- Technical/scientific authority may challenge assumptions or evidence without owning the final capital decision.
- Procurement authority is not automatically capital authority.
- Budget ownership does not eliminate escalation requirements for high-risk categories.

## Independent assurance maturity

The CCF requires a clear distinction among first-line control ownership, second-line risk/compliance challenge, and independent assurance. The final 2026 maturity choice remains a canon decision unless separately locked.

### Historical working implementation assumption — NONCONTROLLING

Until that decision is finalized, architecture should support a **small internal assurance capability with co-sourcing**, reporting functionally to the Audit & Compliance Committee and administratively through an appropriate senior enterprise role.

This model allows:

- independent reviews of selected financial, technology, compliance, and operational-control areas;
- specialist co-sourcing for areas beyond internal capability;
- direct escalation to the Audit & Compliance Committee;
- separation from the Trust/Risk/Compliance second line;
- gradual maturation as ARU, BS&T, Red Wash, and other physical operations increase enterprise complexity.

The CCF must not assume that internal audit performs management controls or owns remediation. Management owns controls and remediation; independent assurance evaluates and reports.

## Governance artifacts implied by this model

The eventual governance-document set should include at minimum:

- board charter/governance guidelines;
- five standing committee charters;
- delegation-of-authority policy and matrix;
- reserved-matters schedule;
- capital-investment governance standard;
- conflicts/related-party policy;
- internal assurance charter if/when the function is canonically established;
- enterprise risk-management policy;
- policy-governance standard;
- subsidiary-governance and intercompany standard;
- acquisition/integration governance standard.
