## 2. Financing history design

### Growth financing — PROVISIONAL 2020/2021 event

Sable Harbor’s first meaningful institutional growth financing occurs around the survival-to-scale transition following The Crossing. The financing should not rewrite the Crossing as an investor rescue: the operational/product breakthrough remains internally earned. Capital follows demonstrated remote-deployment viability and helps fund repeatability, hiring, security/reliability investment, and commercial scaling.

The lead investor is a technology/growth firm that understands vertical enterprise software. Its board representative is commercially disciplined and pushes for repeatability, gross-margin improvement, scalable onboarding, and clearer product boundaries. The investor is not hostile to services; it wants services to reinforce product adoption rather than become uncontrolled bespoke engineering.

### 2022 industrial growth financing — LOCKED DIRECTION / PROVISIONAL economics

The larger 2022 financing introduces a long-duration industrial investor. The investor understands mining, infrastructure, industrial technology, or complex physical operations and is willing to support a company whose future may extend beyond pure SaaS economics.

This capital supports the next phase of Sable Harbor’s growth while creating a legitimate board-level strategic tension:

- technology-growth capital emphasizes repeatability, software economics, focus, and scalable products;
- industrial capital accepts longer-duration experimentation and selected asset/operating exposure when it creates strategic advantage;
- founders retain meaningful influence and operating authority;
- independent directors mediate enterprise interest rather than serving either constituency.

Neither investor controls the company.

## 3. Balanced preferred-governance philosophy — LOCKED

Financing documents should ultimately reflect balanced protective rights rather than unilateral founder or investor control.

Appropriate mechanisms may include:

- negotiated board-designation rights while ownership thresholds are maintained;
- customary protective provisions for fundamental changes;
- information and inspection rights;
- pro-rata participation rights;
- consent requirements for extraordinary transactions or changes to preferred rights;
- sunset/termination provisions tied to ownership or conversion;
- no investor veto over ordinary-course product, hiring, customer, or operational decisions;
- no founder mechanism that makes board oversight merely ceremonial.

Exact legal terms require later capitalization work and should reconcile with the enterprise financial model.

## 4. Committee membership architecture

Exact rosters may evolve, but the following principles apply:

- **Audit & Compliance:** chaired by Calder; management directors do not control the committee; finance/accounting competence is explicit.
- **Compensation & Human Capital:** independent/non-management majority; CEO does not determine his own compensation.
- **Governance & Nominating:** led by independent directors and responsible for board succession and independence assessment.
- **Finance & Investment:** includes strong capital/industrial competence and can include investor directors without becoming an investor veto forum.
- **Technology & Operations:** includes independent technology/security competence plus industrial/operational perspective; management participates by invitation and reporting responsibility.

## 5. Historical governance evolution

The nine-seat 2026 board should be the result of maturation, not the company’s original structure.

- **2016–2019:** founder-dominated governance appropriate to a small services-led company.
- **2020–2021:** first institutional investor representation and initial formalization after The Crossing.
- **2022:** larger financing broadens the board and increases finance/risk discipline.
- **2023:** professionalization accelerates as security, trust, vendor, finance, and governance functions mature; Jon transitions away from executive management while retaining governance connection.
- **2024:** Kincaid becomes independent chair as the CEO/Chair roles separate and Atlas Meridian restarts under a more formal governance environment.
- **2025–2026:** committee architecture expands in response to AI/product governance, physical operations, acquisitions, capital intensity, and enterprise-control complexity.

This history is intended to generate realistic minutes, charter revisions, committee formation records, director appointments, policy approvals, and governance exceptions later. Those artifacts should reflect the state that existed at the time rather than retroactively applying the 2026 model to earlier years.
