# SABLE HARBOR — PEOPLE & CULTURE DOCTRINE

## Purpose

People & Culture exists to make Sable Harbor an exceptional place for exceptional people to do serious work over a long career. **Continuity is capital.** Experience, judgment, relationships, craft, and institutional memory should compound rather than be treated as interchangeable labor.

## Service doctrine

The necessary HR plumbing—payroll, benefits, leave/accommodations, required-training administration, employee relations/investigations, separations, and records—should be nearly invisible when healthy. One human front door is preferred. Self-service removes friction; it never becomes a shield that prevents human help. Recurring pain is a defect. Default to fewer processes and fewer mandatory steps.

P&C owns the employee experience of mandatory learning; the function creating the legal/security/safety/other requirement owns the substantive reason and content authority.

**Never give with one hand and take with the other.** Benefits and programs are tested for friction in money, time, hassle, and stigma from the lived perspective of the least senior employee affected.

## Work and workplace

**Work where the work works best.** Hybrid work is genuine where the role permits it. There is no arbitrary office quota substituting for judgment. Employees are expected to recognize when presence materially improves collaboration, mentoring, coordination, or human interaction. The office must earn the commute. Free on-premise parking is part of the Sacramento workplace principle where driving is a normal commute mode; useful transit support is encouraged as well.

## Talent philosophy

Sable Harbor seeks proven masters, high-slope early-career talent, unconventional excellence conventional screens miss, and people who know something Sable Harbor does not.

Talent cycle: **Spot → Assess → Evaluate → Recruit → Develop → Run.** Talent scouting is a prestigious rotational capability for sufficiently mature practitioners, including people outside P&C. The side door creates visibility, not a lower hiring bar.

Once Sable Harbor asks a candidate for meaningful human time, Sable Harbor owes a human conclusion. Substantive candidates receive real, respectful feedback if not selected.

## Selection flow

1. open application, employee nomination, or scout-created candidate record;
2. initial screen;
3. 45–60 minute domain-capable human conversation;
4. 90–120 minute real-work encounter;
5. one standardized exploration assessment chosen to answer a real unknown;
6. reference/placement work where useful;
7. small selection conference using independent notes and a clear decision owner;
8. rapid personal offer or candid human closure.

### Exploration assessment library

- **The Unknown** — fixed fictional systems-failure investigation with limited scripted questions and final known/unknown/next-action briefing.
- **The Build** — fixed physical-materials construction problem with a mid-exercise payload change, test, and debrief.
- **The Teach** — candidate teaches a non-job subject they genuinely know, no slides, followed by fixed questions and a deliberate misunderstanding to repair.
- **The Hand-Off** — candidate inherits a fixed project, interviews a scripted former owner holding undisclosed facts, then briefs the sponsor.
- **Judgment Under Trade-Offs** — fixed operating choice among three viable options, followed by new evidence and a second decision.
- **Critique** — fixed polished recommendation containing genuine strengths and flaws; candidate challenges the scripted author and recommends proceed/modify/stop.
- **Prioritization** — fixed Monday-morning issue board sorted into do-now/delegate/schedule/not-today, then reprioritized after a curveball.
- **Self-Correction** — fixed case decision, new evidence, revised decision, and explanation of what changed.

Packets, timing, prompts, and assessor scripts are standardized.

## Offers, compensation, and ownership

Sable Harbor offers what it believes is fair if the candidate accepts immediately. Negotiation skill does not determine compensation; new information may correct a bad initial offer. Salary-history games are rejected.

There is **no up-or-out system**. Professional and technical tracks may out-earn management. Management is a separate profession, not the required destination for status or pay. Counteroffers should be rare because compensation health is reviewed before resignation forces the issue.

Long-duration value creation should produce long-duration employee economic participation. Direction: strong cash pay, meaningful profit participation, and broad employee ownership through a conservatively structured ESOP or similar trust rather than a debt-heavy ownership transaction. Employee ownership does not displace Board governance or management authority.

## Careers and mobility

Internal mobility is a right to explore. Current managers may consult but may not silently veto or hoard talent. Employees may deepen professionally, manage, teach, rotate, change business lines, or change geography.

Ordinary work feedback stays lightweight and candid: **How is the work going?** and **Where could you go from here?** A separate Next Chapter conversation explores curiosity, latent strengths, new builds, rotations, and small experiments. No surprise annual files and no stack-ranking spreadsheet culture.

Each employee has access to a P&C talent partner outside the line manager for fit/mobility problems. High-performing team chemistry may be preserved, but teams remain permeable and chemistry creates no clique immunity.

## Management formation

Management is a profession. Prospective managers receive real formation in law, compensation, mobility, hiring, the talent cycle, judgment, and difficult human situations, with practical simulations rather than slideware. Management appointment is reversible without stigma; returning to a professional track is not failure.

Talent/scout education occurs at multiple leadership levels. Strong candidates may enter deeper scout qualification and a roughly 12-month rotational scouting tour.

## Learning

P&C owns opportunity, access, mobility, and career architecture. J2 Education owns education as a professional discipline. Businesses own learning through actual work. Learning time is work.

## Workforce continuity

Hiring is a long-duration capital decision. Overhiring is a management risk.

Before any reduction, Sable Harbor conducts a continuity review across the enterprise: disappearing work, transferable capability, retraining, redeployment, relocation, and whether external hiring should pause. A continuity reserve may fund productive bridging so local budgets do not punish enterprise retention.

**Automation creates capacity, not casualties.** Sable Harbor does not eliminate productive people merely to polish a margin or because automation made current tasks cheaper. Productivity first raises the question: what more can these people do?

If reduction is genuinely unavoidable, reasonable reversible levers, redeployment, retraining, and visible leadership sacrifice precede the irreversible action. Selection follows work/capability, not convenience. Communication is candid. Severance/benefits and placement support are meaningful and dignified. A RIF is a management necessity/failure, not an employee moral failure.

## Succession and listening

Succession is capital preservation. Critical roles should not depend on one human. The purpose is role resilience and capability development, not a secret chosen-one list.

P&C rejects the employee panopticon. Listening relies on voluntary human channels, small groups, stay/exit conversations, manager presence, P&C relationships, and material escalation—not passive behavioral surveillance.

## Anti-bureaucracy rule

P&C has a standing duty to challenge bureaucracy, including its own. Care may not become paternalism. A process that cannot explain the work, legal, safety, continuity, or human problem it solves carries the burden of proof.
