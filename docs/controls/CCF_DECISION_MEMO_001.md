# SABLE HARBOR — CCF DECISION MEMO 001

**Date:** September 2, 2026  
**Purpose:** Identify only decisions that materially shape the corporate world and now block clean population of governance-level controls.  
**Not requested here:** control wording, sample sizes, evidence lists, framework crosswalks, policy prose, system configurations, or implementation minutiae.

## Decision 1 — 2026 corporate/legal architecture

**Why it is now blocking:** CCF applicability, legal/regulatory obligations, intercompany controls, board authority, acquisition integration, financial reporting, treasury, tax, and operating-company governance all need to know what Sable Harbor legally owns and what is merely a product/program/division.

**Recommended default:** Use a U.S. parent corporation as the controlling company, with a deliberately limited set of operating subsidiaries only where liability, regulation, acquisition history, or physical operations justify them. Keep Foundry/Foundry Field, Willow, Atlas Meridian, Cradle, and Advisory as internal product/program/business-line structures unless a later transaction requires a separate entity. Preserve ARU as an acquired legal operating company during integration. Use a separate Pale Sun/Red Wash operating subsidiary structure for mine ownership/operation rather than placing Red Wash directly inside the software parent.

**User decision needed:** Approve this structural principle, modify it, or require a different parent/subsidiary philosophy. Exact entity names/jurisdictions can be populated after the principle is approved.

## Decision 2 — Board and committee model

**Why it is now blocking:** Governance controls need an actual oversight body, reserved matters, risk oversight, financial-reporting oversight, security/compliance oversight, executive compensation/people oversight, and a place for Jon Bell's continuing governance role.

**Recommended default:** A conventional growth-stage private-company board with a majority of non-management directors by 2026 and three standing committees:

- Audit & Risk Committee — financial reporting, external audit/assurance, enterprise risk, compliance, whistleblower oversight, and major control deficiencies;
- Compensation & People Committee — executive compensation, succession, senior leadership, culture/people risk;
- Technology, Safety & Security Committee — cybersecurity, product/AI governance, operational technology, major incidents, and physical operating/safety risk.

The full board retains strategy, major capital, acquisitions/dispositions, financing, CEO appointment, and other reserved matters. Jon Bell remains a director unless later canon says otherwise.

**User decision needed:** Approve the three-committee model, prefer a leaner two-committee model, or specify another board structure.

## Decision 3 — Capital authority and delegation philosophy

**Why it is now blocking:** Existing canon explicitly leaves capital authority open. Procurement, treasury, M&A, Red Wash, Cradle, ARU integration, major vendor commitments, emergency spending, and model-supported capital decisions cannot have credible controls without a delegation philosophy.

**Recommended default:** Tiered delegated authority with escalating dollar/risk thresholds: operating managers -> business-line leaders -> CFO/CEO-level management -> board. High-risk categories can escalate regardless of dollar value. Atlas Meridian can inform a capital decision but never approve it. Field operating owners can stop or constrain operations for immediate safety/operating reasons without holding capital authority.

**User decision needed:** Approve the philosophy. Exact thresholds should be designed later and do not require user micromanagement unless a threshold changes the narrative materially.

## Decision 4 — Independent assurance / internal audit maturity in 2026

**Why it is now blocking:** The CCF needs to know whether management self-assessment, compliance, and independent internal audit are separate functions. This materially changes governance, issue escalation, board oversight, and the eventual relationship with external SOC auditors.

**Recommended default:** Sable Harbor has a small second-line Trust/Risk/Compliance capability by 2026 but does **not** yet have a large mature standalone Internal Audit department. The Audit & Risk Committee has authorized a co-sourced/internal-assurance program that performs targeted independent reviews, with a plan to mature into a dedicated function as the company and physical operations grow. This preserves credible imperfect maturity and avoids designing the company around NAILEX.

**User decision needed:** Approve co-sourced/early internal audit, choose a mature standalone Internal Audit function, or choose no formal internal audit yet.
