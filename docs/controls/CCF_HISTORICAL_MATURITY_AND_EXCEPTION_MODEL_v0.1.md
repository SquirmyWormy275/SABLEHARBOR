# SABLE HARBOR — CCF HISTORICAL MATURITY, EXCEPTION & REMEDIATION MODEL v0.1

**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Date:** September 2, 2026  
**Status:** FOUNDATION POPULATION

Sable Harbor must remain auditable/playable through time. The 2026 control state must not be projected backward onto 2016–2025.

## 1. Control maturity states

Each common control and local implementation can carry a maturity state independent of whether the control exists.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `absent` | No recognizable control design for the objective. |
| `ad_hoc` | People perform useful protective activity, but ownership/process/evidence are inconsistent. |
| `repeatable` | A recurring practice exists and is generally understood, but documentation/coverage is incomplete. |
| `defined` | Ownership, procedure, evidence and scope are documented and normally followed. |
| `managed` | Performance, exceptions, metrics and changes are monitored and governed. |
| `integrated` | Control is embedded across systems/business processes with structured inheritance, reliable evidence and coordinated monitoring. |
| `optimized` | Continuous improvement/automation is mature and validated; this state should be rare and never assumed merely because tooling exists. |

A mature control may still fail. Maturity describes institutionalization, not guaranteed operating effectiveness.

## 2. Corporate maturity eras

### 2016–2017 — founder/services era
- governance concentrated with founders;
- customer contracts, finance, access, security, project delivery and data handling rely heavily on direct knowledge and manual review;
- strong practical competence but limited policy/control formalization;
- most controls `ad_hoc` to `repeatable`;
- evidence exists naturally in contracts, emails, files, invoices, repositories and customer deliverables but is fragmented.

### 2018–2019 — internal tooling and early productization
- Foundry Field precursor creates repeatable engineering/data patterns;
- basic source control, change review, customer onboarding and financial processes become more structured;
- investor readiness begins increasing governance/finance discipline;
- important exceptions remain founder-approved and poorly centralized;
- selected controls reach `defined`; enterprise governance remains mostly `repeatable`.

### 2020 — The Crossing
- severe operating pressure intentionally creates a mixed control state;
- remote deployment, data integration and customer support rapidly mature because survival depends on them;
- emergency changes, temporary access, vendor choices and process workarounds increase;
- some controls improve dramatically while others accumulate debt;
- historical simulation should contain compensating controls, expired waivers that linger, incomplete evidence and retrospective cleanup without caricaturing incompetence.

### 2021 — post-Crossing scaling
- repeatable deployments and product operations solidify;
- hiring, access, vendor, support, engineering and finance volumes expose prior manual processes;
- policy and role formalization begins but local/custom commitments proliferate;
- many technology controls become `defined`; governance/ERM/assurance are still developing.

### 2022 — institutional capital, Emberline and reliability pressure
- financing increases board/finance discipline;
- Project Emberline increases R&D/vendor/data variation;
- major reliability incident drives stronger incident, change, security and service-operations investment;
- acquisition/physical-operation capability is not yet mature;
- controls show credible unevenness rather than uniform progression.

### 2023 — “we have to grow up”
- formal security, trust, IT, vendor management, finance, contracting, documentation and compliance structures emerge;
- enterprise policy hierarchy and control ownership begin to become coherent;
- independent chair/professional governance transition begins;
- first systematic control-monitoring activities appear;
- key enterprise controls move toward `defined`/`managed`.

### 2024 — Atlas reboot and independent governance
- Kincaid becomes chair and CEO/Chair roles separate;
- Atlas model/data governance becomes a new control family with intentionally developing maturity;
- model validation, data rights and human-oversight controls exist but remain imperfect;
- governance, security and financial controls mature further.

### 2025 — expansion pressure
- more structured common controls, vendor tiering, evidence collection and policy governance;
- physical/industrial opportunity creates pressure for field qualification, environmental, asset and capital governance;
- assurance/control monitoring grows, but does not magically eliminate historical exceptions.

### 2026 — diversified enterprise
- five standing board committees, selective legal subsidiaries, formal CCF, stronger capital governance and maturing assurance;
- ARU/BS&T acquisition and Red Wash/Pale Sun introduce significant local-control overlays;
- Sable Harbor common controls are generally `defined` to `managed`; selected technology/finance controls may be `integrated`;
- acquired/physical/new-venture controls range from `repeatable` to `managed` depending on history;
- Atlas, Cradle, Pale Sun and Advisory retain differing maturity profiles.

## 3. Exception taxonomy

Exceptions are first-class records, not footnotes.

- **Deviation:** control expected to operate but a specific occurrence did not conform.
- **Waiver:** authorized temporary departure from design or requirement before/during operation.
- **Risk acceptance:** accountable authority accepts residual exposure after analysis.
- **Compensating-control arrangement:** alternate control(s) reduce risk where normal design is unavailable or inappropriate.
- **Emergency exception:** temporary deviation under defined emergency authority, requiring retrospective review.
- **Inherited exception:** weakness/variation enters Sable Harbor through acquisition or new operating boundary.
- **Structural local variation:** intentionally different design that is not a deficiency when documented and approved.

## 4. Exception lifecycle

Every exception record must carry:

1. affected common control and local implementation;
2. precise scope/population/system/location;
3. business reason and cause;
4. incremental risk and affected objectives;
5. start date and discovery date if different;
6. accountable owner;
7. approval/risk-acceptance authority;
8. compensating controls;
9. expiration/review date;
10. remediation linkage if remediation is intended;
11. status and escalation state;
12. closure criteria and evidence;
13. historical record after closure.

Expired exceptions do not disappear. An expired-but-unresolved exception becomes an escalation condition.

## 5. Issue and remediation severity

CCF issue severity considers several dimensions rather than a single framework label:

- actual/potential financial impact;
- customer/service impact;
- security/privacy impact;
- operating/safety/environmental consequence;
- legal/regulatory/contractual consequence;
- duration and population breadth;
- recurrence/systemic nature;
- detectability;
- management awareness and response;
- compensating controls;
- likelihood of escalation across entities/business lines.

Suggested enterprise labels: `critical`, `high`, `moderate`, `low`, with locally meaningful descriptors retained where required.

## 6. Remediation lifecycle

`identified -> triaged -> owner_assigned -> plan_approved -> in_progress -> validation_pending -> closed`

Alternative terminal states require explicit authority: `risk_accepted`, `superseded_by_design`, `merged`, `not_an_issue`.

Closure requires evidence that addresses the defined closure criteria. Completion of a task is not automatically remediation validation.

## 7. Governance escalation

- Critical enterprise or physical safety issues escalate promptly to executive management and relevant board committee/full board according to consequence.
- Material financial reporting/control issues route to Audit & Compliance.
- Capital/investment governance failures route to Finance & Investment and potentially full board.
- Technology/security/AI/operational-technology issues route to Technology & Operations as appropriate.
- Executive conduct/compensation matters can route to Compensation & Human Capital and/or Governance & Nominating with conflict-aware handling.
- No committee assignment prevents the full board from receiving a matter that crosses reserved-matter or materiality thresholds.

## 8. Historical evidence behavior

For simulation/benchmarking, evidence quality evolves with maturity:

- `ad_hoc`: emails, spreadsheets, tickets, repository history, informal approvals;
- `repeatable`: recurring templates/reports with inconsistent completeness;
- `defined`: controlled forms, registers, workflows and review records;
- `managed`: metrics, exception registers, aging, governance reporting;
- `integrated`: system-linked records and reliable population generation.

Do not manufacture perfect evidence in early years. Also do not make early years implausibly chaotic: competent people produce useful records even before a formal CCF exists.
