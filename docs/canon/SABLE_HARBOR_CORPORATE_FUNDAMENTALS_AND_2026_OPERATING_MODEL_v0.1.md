## 0. Document purpose and decision discipline

This document turns the accepted Sable Harbor history into a first executable present-day operating model. It is deliberately more concrete than an outline: it proposes headcount, offices, reporting lines, funding, ownership, revenue, customers, organizational units, and risks that can later generate coherent synthetic records.

The decision states are:

- **LOCKED:** accepted canon inherited from the architecture handover.
- **PROVISIONAL:** the recommended working default. It should be treated as true for modeling until reviewed, but it may still be revised without retconning locked canon.
- **OPEN:** explicitly unresolved. No downstream generator should silently invent a permanent answer.

All new numerical values in this version are **PROVISIONAL** unless expressly marked otherwise. The numbers are designed to reconcile with the locked history, not to imply that the user has already approved them.

### 0.1 Governing design principle

Sable Harbor must exist as a coherent company independently of any audit exercise. Its systems, contracts, controls, workarounds, evidence, and failures should arise from ordinary operations. Assurance scenarios may observe the company, but they must not dictate the company’s underlying history.

# 2. Legal and corporate structure

## CF-001 — PROVISIONAL

### 2.1 Recommended structure

#### Sable Harbor, Inc.

**Working canonical ID:** `ORG-SH-0001`  
**Form:** Delaware C corporation  
**Headquarters:** Sacramento, California  
**Role:** Parent, primary operating company, employer, owner of commercial intellectual property, contracting party for normal commercial customers, and borrower under the corporate debt facility.

The recommended history is:

1. Sable Harbor was incorporated as a California corporation in Sacramento in 2016.
2. During the 2022 Series B financing, it completed a statutory conversion or reincorporation into Delaware.
3. The Delaware company is the continuing corporate enterprise and remains registered to do business in California and the other states where it has offices or employees.

This preserves the locked Sacramento incorporation story while producing a conventional institutional-capital structure by 2022.

#### Sable Harbor Federal Systems, LLC

**Working canonical ID:** `ORG-SH-FED-0001`  
**Form:** Wholly owned Delaware limited liability company, registered in New Mexico and California  
**Formation:** July 2026  
**Role:** Pale Sun contracting vehicle and eventual employer or secondment recipient for personnel working inside a segregated federal or regulated boundary.

Federal Systems is initially a controlled shell with a small operating team. It does not automatically create a FedRAMP environment, federal authorization, cleared workforce, or compliant system boundary. Its purpose is to avoid signing federal-facing contracts or commingling restricted program costs before the company understands the actual information and authorization requirements.

#### Emberline

Emberline remains a business unit inside Sable Harbor, Inc. It is not a subsidiary in the baseline model.

That decision preserves the program’s intended ambiguity. Emberline can behave like a venture, consulting practice, or laboratory without gaining an artificially clean legal identity that its operations do not yet justify.

#### International operations

No foreign subsidiary is included in v0.1. International customers contract with Sable Harbor, Inc.; limited local activity is supported through approved contractors, employer-of-record arrangements, and customer travel.

This is deliberately temporary. A Canadian or Australian entity may become economically and legally justified after customer geography, employee location, tax exposure, and data-localization requirements are modeled.

### 2.2 Why this structure is plausible

- The 2016 California origin remains intact.
- A 2022 Delaware conversion aligns with the locked institutional-capital event.
- One principal operating company avoids premature subsidiary complexity.
- Federal Systems creates an appropriate legal and accounting seam for Pale Sun without pretending that a legal entity alone establishes a secure boundary.
- Emberline remains close enough to the commercial company to share people, tools, and customers, which explains both its usefulness and its governance problems.

### 2.3 Downstream consequences

This proposal creates natural synthetic records involving:

- articles, board approvals, and state registrations;
- a 2022 legal conversion;
- intercompany services and intellectual-property licensing for Federal Systems;
- separate federal cost accounting and vendor approval;
- contractor and employer-of-record populations;
- ambiguity about whether particular Emberline costs are R&D, cost of revenue, or product development;
- customer-contract assignment and lender-consent questions;
- system-boundary and identity-segregation work for Pale Sun.

### 2.4 OPEN items

- Exact 2016 legal name and charter number.
- Exact 2022 conversion mechanism.
- Whether core IP is owned directly by Sable Harbor, Inc. or placed in a separate IP entity.
- Whether Federal Systems ultimately becomes the system owner, prime contractor, or subcontractor.
- Whether Canada, Australia, or another jurisdiction requires a subsidiary in 2027.

### 4.2 Sacramento

Sacramento remains the legal and cultural headquarters rather than a symbolic mailing address. It contains:

- the CEO and President/COO offices;
- finance, legal, people, procurement, and corporate administration;
- most of Security, Trust, Corporate IT, and Enterprise Risk;
- board and executive meeting facilities;
- a smaller product and commercial presence;
- the official records function.

The office should feel operational and industrial, not like a generic coastal startup headquarters.

### 4.3 Reno

Reno is the primary technical and service-delivery hub. It grew from Nevada customer work and the practical need to be close to mining operations without locating the entire company in a field town.

Reno houses the majority of:

- Foundry Field engineering;
- integration and connector development;
- customer operations and SRE;
- implementation engineering;
- platform product management;
- technical training and deployment labs.

Reno is also where legacy architecture and modern platform practices collide most visibly.

### 4.4 Elko

Elko is not a sales outpost. It is the place where software meets mine-site reality.

Its personnel include:

- field implementation leads;
- mining-systems analysts;
- technical support engineers with site access;
- training specialists;
- domain translators who understand dispatch, maintenance, geology, and plant operations.

Elko employees are likely to distrust corporate processes that do not survive actual customer conditions.

### 4.5 Tucson

Tucson is the center of Atlas Meridian’s mining-science and geology capability. It also supports copper-market customers and technical recruiting.

The office combines:

- geologists;
- mine engineers;
- metallurgists;
- mining economists;
- applied scientists;
- Atlas product and model-validation personnel.

### 4.6 Charleston

Charleston remains Emberline’s center. It has a small-office identity, local vendor ecosystem, field equipment, and a high degree of operational autonomy.

The office is productive and commercially relevant, but its procurement, data, and prototype practices are less standardized than those of the main company.

### 4.7 Albuquerque

Albuquerque is the recommended Pale Sun location because it creates a credible intersection among uranium history, technical talent, federal programs, national-laboratory ecosystems, and western mining.

The office begins with 12 people and a deliberately limited network and vendor boundary. It is not yet a production federal environment. The company must decide what information it will handle before finalizing its security architecture.

### 4.8 Remote-work model

Sable Harbor is hybrid, but not remote-first. Most roles are assigned to a physical hub because mine-site work, secure systems, customer implementations, hardware labs, and cross-functional operations benefit from location.

The 17 remote employees are exceptions based on scarce expertise or customer geography rather than a universal employment model.

- Five scheduled board meetings per year.
- Audit, Risk & Trust meets at least quarterly and after material incidents.
- Compensation & Talent meets three times per year plus grant approvals.
- Technology, Product & Responsible AI meets quarterly during Atlas launch and Pale Sun formation.

### 7.4 Governance tension

Three founders on a seven-seat board preserve founding influence. Two investor directors and two independents prevent the board from functioning as a founder council.

Jon’s presence is useful but complicated: he can challenge management from institutional memory while lacking current operating accountability.

# 19. Current strategic tensions

## CF-018 — PROVISIONAL

The following tensions are structural rather than personality defects.

### 19.1 Foundry Field funds the future

Foundry Field generates the majority of revenue and almost all product gross profit. Atlas and Pale Sun consume capital and executive attention.

Foundry leaders can reasonably argue that reliability, connector modernization, and customer commitments should receive more investment. Atlas leaders can reasonably argue that failure to invest now would trap the company in integration software.

### 19.2 Services are both strength and trap

Services reveal customer reality and make deployments succeed. They also mask product gaps, reduce margin, and allow sales to promise outcomes the product does not yet support cleanly.

### 19.3 Customer exceptions versus platform coherence

Large customers have legitimate differences in systems, terminology, data rights, security, and mine operations. Treating all variation as “bad customization” is naïve. Treating every request as permanent product behavior is unsustainable.

### 19.4 Crossing speed versus grown-up controls

Veterans remember that emergency action saved the company. Newer leaders remember that undocumented exceptions, weak ownership, and heroics now threaten reliability and trust.

Both positions contain truth.

### 19.5 Field truth versus central consistency

Elko and Charleston teams encounter customer realities that Sacramento or Reno policies may not anticipate. Central teams see risk and duplication that field teams may normalize.

### 19.6 Commercial ambition versus implementation capacity

Mining contracts are large enough that commercial teams feel pressure to accept custom terms. Delivery teams know that a small number of unusually complex commitments can consume a disproportionate share of scarce technical talent.

### 19.7 Atlas precision versus scientific honesty

Customers want decisive scenarios and numbers. Maya’s team insists on uncertainty, provenance, and human review. Sales and customers may interpret those limitations as friction; the scientists view them as product integrity.

### 19.8 Pale Sun reuse versus segregation

Reusing Foundry and Atlas components accelerates Pale Sun. Reusing too much of the commercial environment can contaminate the intended boundary, complicate authorization, and create support obligations the company cannot safely meet.

### 19.9 Growth plan versus capital discipline

The 2024 financing created expectations for growth. Controlled Atlas launch, federal caution, and Foundry reliability spending make the company look more disciplined but less explosive than the original growth case.
