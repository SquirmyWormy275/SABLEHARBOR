# SABLE HARBOR ADVISORY — FINANCIAL OPERATING MODEL

**Document ID:** `SH-ADV-013`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Effective:** September 9, 2026  
**State:** `LOCKED DIRECTION`; quantitative forecasts remain `MODEL_PROPOSED` until separately accepted  
**Owner:** Advisory Finance with President, Sable Harbor Advisory  
**Related:** `SH-ADV-005`; `SH-ADV-009`; enterprise finance platform

## 1. Purpose

This model defines how Advisory should be managed financially as an outcome-based, AI-native professional-services business. It is a management architecture, not a claim that current Sable Harbor financial statements already contain these exact values.

## 2. Revenue streams

Advisory recognizes four distinct external revenue streams:

1. **Committed professional fees** — fixed/milestone fees for accepted matters.
2. **Variable outcome fees** — contingent economics certified under the Value Office methodology.
3. **Managed continuity fees** — recurring professional-service obligations.
4. **Atlas-related professional implementation/support** — Advisory-delivered configuration or professional capability work; Atlas product subscription revenue remains product revenue and should not be disguised as Advisory revenue merely because the same client buys both.

The finance system should preserve product/service separation while allowing consolidated relationship economics.

## 3. Matter P&L

Every matter has an internal economic record containing:

- contracted committed fee;
- variable fee opportunity and probability-weighted forecast;
- Atlas/product charges and ownership;
- direct labor cost;
- contractor/subcontractor cost;
- travel;
- third-party data/model/tool cost;
- compute allocation where material;
- review and Value Office cost;
- claims/warranty/transfer-support reserve;
- collections status;
- realized contribution margin;
- lessons for future pricing.

The client is not billed from this record unless the contract explicitly uses a reimbursable element.

## 4. Backlog

Backlog distinguishes:

- committed signed fee backlog;
- variable fee opportunity not yet earned;
- renewal/managed continuity backlog;
- Atlas product backlog owned by Atlas;
- uncontracted pipeline.

Variable outcome fees are not treated as guaranteed backlog.

## 5. Revenue recognition posture

Accounting treatment remains subject to the enterprise accounting policy and qualified review. Management reporting should be conservative:

- committed fees recognized according to contracted delivery/acceptance pattern;
- variable outcome fees forecast separately and recognized only when required measurement/certification and collectibility thresholds are satisfied under applicable policy;
- material client disputes reduce forecast confidence and may require reserve;
- managed continuity recognized over the service period or accepted milestones as appropriate.

## 6. Capacity model

Advisory capacity is managed by **scarce capability**, not only headcount.

Capacity planning tracks:

- Principal/Distinguished Principal bandwidth;
- operating specialists by domain;
- technical/agent builders;
- independent reviewers;
- Value Office capacity;
- Atlas implementation/support dependency;
- travel/field constraints;
- concurrent Q3/Q4 matter load.

The firm should be willing to turn away work before overloading professional quality.

## 7. Utilization

Utilization is monitored by role and capability as an economic signal. It is not a target to maximize mechanically.

Illustrative sustainable ranges for planning:

| Role | Client/matter load guide |
|---|---:|
| Distinguished/Senior Principal | 40–60% direct matter time |
| Principal | 55–70% |
| Senior Professional | 65–80% |
| Professional | 70–82% |
| Associate Professional | 65–80% |

Time outside matters includes quality review, method development, learning, Atlas feedback, recruiting, relationship development and institutional work. These ranges are planning heuristics, not performance quotas.

## 8. Gross margin and contribution

The firm measures:

- matter gross contribution;
- portfolio contribution after shared professional infrastructure;
- Advisory economic profit after enterprise allocations and required reserves.

It does not manage toward a single margin number regardless of practice mix. Intelligence Capability builds may have different economics from Decision Matters or Operational Excellence interventions.

## 9. Target economics

A mature Advisory portfolio should generally seek:

- high gross contribution on scarce-judgment Decision Matters;
- credible margin on Capability Matters after transfer/support cost;
- outcome-fee upside that rewards genuine measurable value without making base economics dependent on heroic payouts;
- recurring managed-continuity revenue only where clients actually need continuity;
- product pull-through that benefits Sable Harbor without forcing unneeded service attachment.

The purpose is durable professional economics, not maximizing near-term leverage through cheap labor.

## 10. Reserves

Advisory establishes reserves for:

- outcome-fee disputes;
- client claims;
- professional liability;
- transfer/warranty obligations;
- uncollectible receivables;
- significant subcontractor commitments;
- Atlas or third-party service credits allocated to the matter where applicable.

Carry is calculated only after required reserves.

## 11. Working capital

Large matters may require mobilization payments or milestone billing to avoid Sable Harbor financing a client's transformation unnecessarily. Finance monitors:

- DSO;
- unbilled WIP where applicable;
- milestone acceptance delays;
- variable-fee measurement lag;
- retained/held-back amounts;
- subcontractor cash exposure.

## 12. Portfolio concentration

Management reporting shows concentration by:

- client;
- sector;
- geography;
- practice;
- relationship principal;
- outcome-fee exposure;
- single external platform/model dependency;
- single operating-business source of expertise.

Concentration is both a financial and independence risk.

## 13. Compensation pool

Annual cash bonus funding considers:

- firm economic performance;
- matter quality and claims;
- individual professional performance;
- client outcome/transfer evidence;
- institutional contribution;
- commercial stewardship.

Carry follows the separate `SH-ADV-005` calculation and is not mixed into ordinary annual bonus.

## 14. Internal investment

Advisory reinvests deliberately in:

- Atlas product requirements and integrations where responsibility sits with Advisory;
- professional methods;
- evaluation and quality infrastructure;
- training;
- sector/domain capability;
- client-safe benchmark cases;
- knowledge and after-action systems.

Investment is not starved merely because it is not immediately billable.

## 15. Pricing feedback loop

Finance and the Value Office review completed matters to compare:

- quoted addressable value;
- realized value;
- committed/variable mix;
- actual delivery cost;
- risk/claims;
- collection cycle;
- client dependency;
- transfer effort;
- final contribution.

This evidence updates pricing bands and proposal assumptions. The firm should become measurably better at pricing its own work.

## 16. Scenario planning

At minimum, the annual plan includes:

- base case;
- low-demand case;
- outcome-fee underperformance case;
- senior-talent attrition case;
- major claim/cyber disruption case;
- rapid Atlas adoption/product-led case;
- constrained-capacity/high-demand case.

The business should survive without assuming every variable fee pays at target.

## 17. Management dashboard

The monthly Executive Operating Review should see:

- signed committed backlog;
- variable-fee exposure by probability/status;
- pipeline by matter class/practice;
- client concentration;
- utilization/capacity by scarce role;
- contribution margin;
- DSO/collections;
- reserves/claims;
- transfer failures;
- review findings;
- hiring/attrition;
- carry pool forecast after hurdle.

## 18. Financial test

A sophisticated partner or CFO should be able to understand how Advisory makes money without seeing an hourly-rate factory: accept fewer consequential problems, price against value/capability, deploy scarce experts plus Atlas/agents, transfer durable capability, preserve margin through better problem solving, and maintain enough reserves and independence that outcome pricing does not become reckless gambling.
