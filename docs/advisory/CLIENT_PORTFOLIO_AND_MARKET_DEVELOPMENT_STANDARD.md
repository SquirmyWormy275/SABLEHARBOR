# SABLE HARBOR ADVISORY — CLIENT PORTFOLIO AND MARKET DEVELOPMENT STANDARD

**Document ID:** `SH-ADV-015`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Effective:** September 9, 2026  
**State:** `LOCKED DIRECTION`  
**Owner:** President, Sable Harbor Advisory with Practice Leaders  
**Related:** `SH-ADV-004`; `SH-ADV-006`; `SH-ADV-009`

## 1. Market posture

Advisory competes on consequential problem solving, operating credibility, investigative rigor, Atlas Meridian capability and durable transfer. It does not attempt to replicate a generalist consulting catalog across every functional topic.

The market promise is simple: **bring Sable Harbor a consequential problem. The firm will assemble the right human and machine capability around it, intervene where appropriate, prove what changed, and leave the client stronger.**

## 2. Target client profile

Priority clients are institutions where:

- operational or technical complexity is real;
- evidence is fragmented across systems;
- decisions have material physical, financial or institutional consequence;
- generic best-practice decks are insufficient;
- client leaders value truth over preferred conclusions;
- Atlas can create continuing decision capability;
- Sable Harbor can transfer meaningful capability without compromising its own crown jewels.

## 3. Sector posture

The firm may serve multiple sectors, but credentials should initially be strongest where Sable Harbor possesses genuine operating or technical depth, including industrial, resource, logistics, infrastructure, energy, field operations, complex software/industrial systems and adjacent institutional-intelligence problems.

Practice expansion follows evidence of capability, not marketing ambition.

## 4. Relationship model

Every material client relationship has a named Relationship Principal responsible for:

- long-term context;
- relationship integrity;
- coordinated access to Sable Harbor capabilities;
- conflict awareness;
- client feedback;
- avoiding duplicate/uncoordinated pursuits;
- ensuring the client is not treated as personal property.

The Relationship Principal does not automatically become Matter Principal.

## 5. Account planning

Strategic accounts maintain a concise annual account plan in Atlas containing:

- client strategic context;
- known operating/decision priorities;
- current matters and Atlas footprint;
- relationship map;
- potential conflicts;
- client capability maturity;
- where Sable Harbor can credibly help;
- where Sable Harbor should not sell;
- renewal/expansion risks;
- senior relationship actions.

Account plans are not speculative dossiers built from protected information. They use lawful relationship intelligence.

## 6. Pursuit qualification

Before a major proposal, the pursuit owner answers:

1. Is the problem consequential enough for Sable Harbor?
2. Do we have distinctive competence?
3. Can we win without promising a preferred answer?
4. Is the buyer the real decision owner?
5. Can the client support data/access/implementation?
6. Is there a credible commercial model?
7. Does the matter create unacceptable conflict or IP risk?
8. Can Atlas materially improve delivery or transfer?
9. Do we have capacity without degrading existing matters?
10. If we win, will this create a client we want to serve again?

Low-quality pursuits are killed early.

## 7. Proposal governance

Proposals above an internal materiality threshold receive review for:

- problem clarity;
- credentials and staffing truthfulness;
- scope/authority;
- commercial model;
- value claims;
- conflicts;
- Atlas/product representation;
- transfer promise;
- liability/risk terms;
- unsupported marketing assertions.

No proposal may name unavailable senior talent as decoration without a credible role and capacity plan.

## 8. Credentials

Credentials are evidence, not logos. Reusable credential records should state:

- client/sector only where disclosure is permitted;
- problem;
- Sable Harbor role;
- outcome/capability;
- what was measured;
- what was transferred;
- limitations/confidentiality;
- reference rights.

Synthetic/demo cases are clearly labeled as such.

## 9. Thought leadership

Advisory may publish research, methods and sector insight to build reputation. Publication quality should meet the same evidence standard as client work proportionate to context.

Thought leadership may not expose:

- client confidential data;
- Sable Harbor operating-business secrets;
- J2 protected information;
- misleading pseudo-case studies;
- invented market facts presented as observed.

## 10. Cross-sell flywheel

The desired ecosystem is reciprocal:

- Foundry Field exposes and represents operational reality;
- Atlas Meridian investigates and becomes the client's ongoing decision platform;
- Advisory tackles consequential problems and builds/transfers capability;
- Advisory experience generates lawful generalized requirements for Atlas;
- Atlas enables clients to solve more routine problems themselves and escalates harder ones to Advisory;
- strong Advisory relationships create demand for Sable Harbor products where they genuinely fit.

Cross-sell is based on fit, not quotas that force products into irrelevant matters.

## 11. Pipeline stages

Pipeline is controlled through:

- **Signal** — credible need identified;
- **Qualified** — decision owner/problem/fit known;
- **Shaping** — matter framing and commercial architecture in development;
- **Proposed** — formal proposal delivered;
- **Contracting** — commercial intent exists, terms unresolved;
- **Accepted** — signed and matter acceptance complete;
- **Closed-lost/declined** — disposition and reason retained.

Forecasts distinguish signed backlog from probability-weighted pipeline.

## 12. Win/loss review

Material pursuits receive lightweight win/loss review. The firm studies:

- client decision criteria;
- positioning;
- price;
- credentials;
- relationship quality;
- proposal clarity;
- competitive differentiation;
- whether losing was actually the right institutional outcome.

A declined bad-fit matter is not a commercial failure.

## 13. Client feedback

At meaningful milestones and close, the Relationship Principal or independent senior professional seeks client feedback on:

- value;
- judgment quality;
- responsiveness;
- implementation;
- transfer;
- Atlas usability;
- commercial clarity;
- where Sable Harbor created or reduced dependency.

Feedback is captured institutionally, not kept only in a rainmaker's memory.

## 14. Concentration and independence

Market development is constrained by client concentration, sector concentration, relationship-principal concentration and outcome-fee exposure. No commercial leader may rationalize unhealthy dependence because a single client is prestigious.

## 15. Reputation standard

The firm should be known for work it can actually defend. Advisory would rather have a smaller portfolio of consequential, referable matters than a broad catalog of generic offerings sustained by marketing language the delivery system cannot distinguish in practice.
