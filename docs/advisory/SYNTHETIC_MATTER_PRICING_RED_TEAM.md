# SABLE HARBOR ADVISORY — SYNTHETIC MATTER PRICING RED TEAM

**Document ID:** `SH-ADV-010`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Effective:** September 9, 2026  
**State:** `MODEL_VALIDATION`; scenarios are synthetic and non-canonical client examples  
**Owner:** Advisory Finance / Value Office  
**Related:** `SH-ADV-002`; `SH-ADV-009`

## 1. Purpose

This document stress-tests the Advisory pricing architecture against realistic synthetic matters. It is not a rate card and does not represent actual clients. The objective is to identify where value pricing, the 60/40 default, capability milestones, Atlas licensing, and independent outcome certification succeed or fail.

## 3. Case 1 — haul-network optimization

### Problem
A resource operator has excessive empty miles, poor dispatch synchronization and avoidable detention across a multi-terminal network.

### Baseline
Verified annual avoidable operating value: **$18M**.

### Quote
Target fee at 9.2%: **$1.65M**.

- committed: $990K;
- variable: $660K;
- Atlas license: separately priced.

### Measurement
Variable fee tied to normalized cost per delivered ton, empty-mile ratio, detention and on-time delivery after adjusting for volume mix, diesel index and force majeure.

### Red-team finding
The model works. It fails if the team simply takes a percentage of gross savings without normalizing fuel and volume changes the client would have received anyway.

## 4. Case 2 — mine productivity intervention

### Problem
An underground operation seeks greater effective production through better development sequencing, maintenance coordination and shift handoff.

### Value
Verified addressable annual margin improvement: **$42M**.

### Quote
**$2.7M**, 65% committed / 35% variable.

### Guardrail
No variable fee is earned from production gains that correlate with deterioration in safety, ground-control compliance, equipment inspection completion, environmental compliance or other agreed countermetrics.

### Red-team finding
Outcome pricing is credible only when the metric is multidimensional. A single production metric creates a perverse incentive.

## 5. Case 3 — reclamation-function build

### Problem
Client needs a modern reclamation organization, planning cadence, governance, cost model, contractor controls, regulatory commitments system, Atlas workflows and trained staff.

### Quote
**$1.2M** capability matter:

- 20% mobilization/design;
- 30% operating model;
- 25% Atlas/workflow build;
- 15% supervised client operation;
- 10% final transfer certificate.

### Red-team finding
Attempting to take a percentage of estimated future reclamation savings would be intellectually weak. Capability acceptance is the right commercial model.

## 6. Case 4 — intelligence capability build

### Problem
A diversified industrial client wants its own decision-intelligence/J2-like function.

### Quote
**$2.8M professional fee plus Atlas licensing**.

### Acceptance tests
The client must independently demonstrate:

- question intake;
- collection/evidence governance;
- structured analysis;
- challenge/review;
- authority boundaries;
- escalation;
- retention;
- executive brief production;
- operation of transferred agents;
- documented correction of a deliberately injected evidence conflict.

### Red-team finding
A pile of decks and org charts is not completion. Pricing should keep meaningful economics at risk until the client can run the function.

## 7. Case 5 — acquisition investigation

### Problem
Client asks whether to acquire a $350M industrial asset.

### Quote
**$1.75M fixed Decision Matter**.

### Why no success fee
Sable Harbor must be economically indifferent between “buy,” “do not buy,” and “insufficient evidence.” Any fee contingent on transaction close corrupts the investigative premise.

### Red-team finding
The outcome model has an explicit limit: not every consequential matter should have outcome participation.

## 8. Case 6 — Atlas self-service deployment

### Problem
Client wants Atlas Meridian without an ongoing Advisory engagement.

### Quote
Illustrative **$450K annual enterprise subscription + $300K implementation**.

### Red-team finding
Advisory must not artificially attach a large consulting team to a product deployment the Atlas product organization can support directly. Product economics and professional economics remain visible.

## 9. Case 7 — distressed logistics turnaround

### Problem
Network instability is causing an estimated $70M annual combination of lost contribution, penalties and avoidable premium freight.

### Quote
**$4.2M**, 50/50 committed-variable.

### Why more variable
Client execution, labor availability and external carrier behavior strongly affect realized value. Sable Harbor shares more performance risk while retaining a committed fee for concentrated senior capacity.

### Red-team finding
A rigid 60/40 rule would be inferior. The policy correctly permits movement within a controlled range.

## 10. Case 8 — low-value/high-complexity matter

### Problem
A $1.5M operational decision requires a rare geotechnical/logistics combination and senior judgment over three weeks.

### Quote
**$425K fixed**.

### Red-team finding
Pure percentage-of-value pricing would underpay scarce expertise and encourage the firm to reject important smaller matters. Minimum fee and Decision Matter pricing solve this.

## 11. Case 9 — failed transfer

### Problem
Client buys a $1M capability build but repeatedly fails operating acceptance because named owners are not assigned and required source systems remain unavailable.

### Commercial result
Earlier milestones properly earned; final 25% remains unearned. Contract invokes client-dependency clauses for schedule relief, but Sable Harbor does not certify a capability that does not work.

### Red-team finding
Transfer-at-risk is commercially painful but essential to credibility.

## 12. Case 10 — $650M value transformation

### Problem
Network redesign and operating transformation with $650M verified multi-year net present value.

### Quote
**$7.5M** total target fee, materially below a naive 2% calculation, with explicit cap and a 70/30 committed-variable split.

### Red-team finding
At very large value, percentage bands are only opening heuristics. Market credibility, marginal contribution and risk caps matter more than mechanically applying a rate.

## 13. Case 11 — independent regulatory review

### Problem
Client requires a decision-grade review of a high-consequence operating control before presenting its position to regulators and lenders.

### Quote
**$900K fixed**, Q3 quality tier.

### Red-team finding
A favorable-outcome contingency would be inappropriate. Independence outweighs alignment rhetoric.

## 14. Case 12 — managed intelligence continuity

### Problem
Client has completed an intelligence capability build but wants ongoing monitoring, model upkeep, agent evaluation, periodic challenge and surge support.

### Quote
Illustrative **$1.8M annual professional service + Atlas subscription**.

### Contracted service
Defines response windows, periodic reviews, monitoring scope, surge capacity and annual reacceptance. It does not promise named full-time bodies or sell 12 months of timesheets.

## 15. Failure modes identified

The pricing architecture should reject or redesign matters where:

- addressable value is inflated from total enterprise value;
- the metric can be gamed through unsafe or short-term behavior;
- attribution is dominated by market prices or client decisions;
- the desired result is a predetermined conclusion;
- the value window is too long to measure credibly;
- client procurement forces hidden hourly economics inconsistent with the promise;
- Atlas licensing is obscured inside professional fees;
- the variable fee is so large that a reasonable reviewer would doubt independence;
- the matter is too small for percentage pricing but still requires rare judgment.

## 16. Conclusion

The model survives the red team when it is treated as a decision framework rather than a slogan. “Outcomes, not hours” means choosing the correct outcome instrument: measurable value where attribution is credible, capability acceptance where the product is a working institution, fixed price where independent judgment is the product, and managed continuity where a persistent service is genuinely required.
