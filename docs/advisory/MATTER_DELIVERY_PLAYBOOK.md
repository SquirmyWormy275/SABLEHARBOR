## 1. Purpose

This playbook defines the repeatable professional lifecycle of an Advisory matter. It is intentionally more operational than the executive operating model. The objective is to make delivery observable, reviewable, transferable and scalable without recreating a consulting pyramid.

## 2. Lifecycle

Every matter moves through seven controlled stages:

1. **Qualify**
2. **Frame**
3. **Mobilize**
4. **Investigate / Build**
5. **Intervene**
6. **Prove**
7. **Transfer / Close**

A matter may loop backward when evidence changes the problem. The lifecycle is not a waterfall fiction.

## 3. Stage 1 — Qualify

Required outputs:

- client identity and decision owner;
- preliminary problem statement;
- conflict/integrity screen;
- competence assessment;
- commercial class;
- preliminary value/capability thesis;
- Atlas/data feasibility;
- acceptance owner;
- go/hold/decline disposition.

No substantive unpaid consulting is performed merely to win work. Enough work is done to determine whether Sable Harbor should accept the matter and what it would be accountable for.

## 4. Stage 2 — Frame

The Matter Principal converts the sales problem into a professional matter charter.

### Matter charter

Must identify:

- consequential question;
- client decision owner;
- outcome or capability state;
- Sable Harbor authorities and explicit non-authorities;
- scope and exclusions;
- decision deadlines;
- evidence/data perimeter;
- hypotheses/alternative explanations where appropriate;
- key risks;
- quality tier;
- review plan;
- commercial metric/value schedule;
- transfer obligation;
- exit conditions.

The charter is signed/accepted before the team optimizes around a mistaken problem definition.

## 5. Stage 3 — Mobilize

The firm assembles the smallest credible team from the common bench.

Typical team composition may include:

- Matter Principal;
- domain operator/practitioner;
- technical/data/agent builder;
- analyst/professional;
- client-side owner;
- Atlas Matter Steward;
- Independent Reviewer;
- Value Office representative where needed.

The team creates:

- RACI/authority map;
- evidence request;
- source register;
- communication cadence;
- issue/question register;
- agent/tool plan;
- security/data handling plan;
- initial workplan;
- transfer plan from day one.

## 6. Stage 4 — Investigate / Build

The matter works from evidence rather than from a predetermined slide storyline.

Required disciplines:

- maintain live issue tree/question register;
- preserve contradictory evidence;
- separate fact from inference;
- record key assumptions;
- use Atlas agent constellations with explicit owners/permissions;
- test material calculations;
- record client dependencies;
- update the matter thesis when evidence changes;
- keep the client informed of material surprises rather than waiting for final presentation theater.

## 7. Working sessions versus deliverable theater

The default client interaction is a working session around evidence, decisions, prototypes, operating changes or capability—not a weekly status deck for its own sake.

A presentation is used when presentation is the right instrument: board decision, executive synthesis, regulator communication, training, or final decision package. Deck production is not the unit of progress.

## 8. Stage 5 — Intervene

Advisory is accountable for more than diagnosis when the contract includes implementation.

Intervention may include:

- operating-process changes;
- decision routines;
- organizational roles;
- control design;
- technical/Atlas configuration;
- agent/workflow deployment;
- training;
- KPI/outcome instrumentation;
- supplier/logistics redesign;
- field pilots;
- governance implementation.

Interventions with physical/safety consequence remain under client operating authority unless explicitly contracted otherwise.

## 9. Change adoption

“Client did not adopt” is not automatically a valid excuse for failed transfer. The engagement design should identify adoption owners, incentives, training, governance, systems and decision rights early.

The team distinguishes:

- Sable Harbor implementation failure;
- client dependency failure;
- jointly controlled failure;
- external/force-majeure effect.

This distinction matters for both professional learning and outcome economics.

## 10. Stage 6 — Prove

Before declaring success, the matter demonstrates the result.

### Measurable Value Matter
Value Office certifies agreed metrics and attribution.

### Capability Matter
Client performs acceptance tests under realistic operating conditions.

### Decision Matter
Independent review confirms the conclusion is decision-grade, evidence-supported and appropriately qualified.

### Managed Continuity
Service performance is measured against contracted service levels/outcomes.

## 11. Transfer readiness

A matter is not ready to close until the client has:

- named owners;
- operating runbooks;
- required access/credentials;
- trained users/operators;
- accepted transferred agents/workflows;
- tests and monitoring;
- known limitations;
- escalation/support path;
- IP/data classification;
- export package;
- governance cadence.

## 12. Stage 7 — Transfer / Close

Required close package:

- executive outcome memo;
- final evidence/conclusion record;
- value or capability certificate;
- client-owned deliverables;
- licensed Atlas/components list;
- transfer certificate;
- unresolved risks/limitations;
- open actions with owners;
- retention/deletion disposition;
- post-matter support terms;
- after-action review.

The matter state in Atlas is then closed. Continuing Managed Continuity becomes a new or renewed matter, not an indefinitely open transformation engagement.

## 13. Matter cadence

Typical cadence for a complex matter:

- daily internal evidence/issue review;
- weekly client working session;
- biweekly Matter Principal quality/economics checkpoint;
- milestone Independent Reviewer challenge;
- monthly executive steering only where the client actually needs it.

Cadence is adapted to the matter; meeting volume is not a quality metric.

## 14. Executive communication standard

Every executive-level matter update should answer:

1. What changed since the last decision point?
2. What do we now know?
3. What do we no longer believe?
4. What decision or action is required?
5. What could make us wrong?
6. What is the expected value/capability effect?
7. What is next?

## 15. Workpaper standard

Material work should be reproducible by another qualified professional with reasonable effort. Workpapers need not be bureaucratic, but they must preserve:

- source;
- method;
- calculation/model version;
- reviewer;
- conclusion;
- unresolved exception;
- linkage to client decision/output.

## 16. Staffing economics

The Matter Principal is expected to use AI/agents and scarce human expertise to reduce unnecessary labor. The client does not receive a lower-quality team because the fee is fixed; Sable Harbor benefits economically when it solves the matter elegantly.

The firm does not add junior staff merely to create leverage ratios or utilization.

## 17. Junior apprenticeship

Associates learn through controlled exposure to:

- source evaluation;
- problem framing;
- client interviews;
- model/agent testing;
- field observation;
- intervention design;
- transfer;
- review challenge.

They are not trained primarily by producing formatting, meeting notes or repetitive spreadsheet labor that a machine can perform better.

## 18. Escalation triggers

Immediate Matter Principal escalation is required for:

- material safety concern;
- evidence suggesting fraud or illegal conduct;
- material confidentiality/security incident;
- client pressure for a predetermined result;
- baseline/outcome dispute affecting fee;
- major model/agent failure;
- newly discovered conflict;
- scope change beyond authority;
- inability to transfer promised capability;
- expected matter loss or claim above threshold.

## 19. Closure quality test

A matter is truly closed when the client can explain what changed, operate what was transferred, distinguish Sable Harbor's retained/licensed capability from its own, and continue without pretending the engagement succeeded merely because the final presentation happened.
