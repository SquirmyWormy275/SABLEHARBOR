## 1. Institutional design

Sable Harbor Advisory is operated as a serious professional-services business, not as a loose collection of experts and not as a sales overlay on Sable Harbor products. It has a single accountable President, a common professional bench, three market-facing practices, matter-level accountability, independent professional review, and explicit separation between business management and professional authority.

The firm is designed to support four simultaneous facts:

1. a person may be exceptionally senior without managing a large organization;
2. matter leadership is not automatically the same thing as relationship ownership;
3. sales credit does not create professional authority;
4. compensation ceiling does not determine line authority.

## 2. President

The President owns the Advisory business as an operating line and is accountable for:

- P&L and capital stewardship;
- annual business plan and risk appetite within enterprise authority;
- workforce and capacity;
- market strategy and client portfolio;
- appointment of senior management roles;
- approval of professional policy changes;
- escalation of reserved matters;
- integrity of the carry plan and compensation architecture;
- ensuring that J2 boundaries are not compromised for commercial advantage.

The President does not have unilateral authority to override Legal, Finance, Security, Risk, Safety, Internal Audit, J2, or enterprise reserved authorities within their established domains.

## 3. Senior professional architecture

Advisory uses two equal-status ladders.

### 3.1 Business-management ladder

| Level | Typical authority | Core accountability |
|---|---|---|
| President | enterprise operating-line authority for Advisory | P&L, strategy, workforce, external commitments |
| Managing Director | portfolio/business segment authority | market portfolio, capacity, commercial discipline, senior client relationships |
| Director | bounded line-management authority | teams, staffing, development, local/sector execution |
| Manager | first-line people/capacity authority | supervision, scheduling, development, operating discipline |

### 3.2 Professional ladder

| Level | Typical authority | Core accountability |
|---|---|---|
| Distinguished Principal | firm-wide professional authority without required line command | exceptional judgment, difficult matters, methodology, reputation |
| Senior Principal | major matter and review authority | complex client outcomes, senior review, professional development |
| Principal | matter leadership and client judgment | Matter Principal service, synthesis, client decision support |
| Senior Professional | independent workstream leadership | evidence, analysis, intervention design, transfer |
| Professional | accountable professional execution | structured analysis, implementation, testing, client transfer |
| Associate Professional | apprenticeship | supervised contribution, evidence discipline, technical craft |

Professional titles are not consolation prizes for people who do not manage. A Distinguished Principal may earn more than a Managing Director and may lead the firm's most consequential client work while holding little or no line authority.

## 4. Former Orientation Officers

A former Orientation Officer may advance without artificial ceiling through the professional ladder, including Distinguished Principal. They may own matters, professional conclusions, review, method, mentoring, and carry.

They may not receive prohibited line authority through title engineering, de facto headcount control, operating budgets, hiring/firing command, standing business-unit control, or equivalent mechanisms. The firm must evaluate the substance of authority, not the label.

If a role becomes ambiguous, the role is narrowed rather than interpreting the anti-line-authority bargain away.

## 5. Practice leadership

The three practices are market and craft communities, not staffing silos:

- **Intelligence Advisory** — Sable Harbor applies investigative capability to consequential client questions.
- **Intelligence Capability** — Sable Harbor helps clients build durable intelligence/J2-like capabilities appropriate to their institution.
- **Operational Excellence** — Sable Harbor improves how clients run physical and technical operations using real operating experience.

Each practice has a Practice Leader appointed by the President. Practice Leaders own market coherence, methods, credentials, talent development, and pipeline quality. They do not own professionals as exclusive resources; the common bench remains firm-wide.

## 6. Common bench and staffing authority

The default staffing rule is **problem first**.

A matter team is assembled from the common bench according to required judgment, operating experience, technical depth, client context, independence needs, and transfer requirements. Utilization targets cannot force a materially unqualified person onto a matter.

Staffing disputes are resolved by the Matter Principal and responsible management leader, with professional quality prevailing over internal utilization convenience.

## 7. Matter roles

Every consequential matter has explicit role assignments:

- **Relationship Principal** — senior commercial/client continuity owner; may or may not be the Matter Principal.
- **Matter Principal** — non-delegable professional accountability for the promise made to the client.
- **Acceptance Principal** — independent acceptance determination before substantive work begins.
- **Independent Reviewer** — required based on risk, novelty, public consequence, regulated exposure, or material outcome economics.
- **Value Office Lead** — independent baseline/outcome certification where variable economics are used.
- **Transfer Owner** — accountable for client capability acceptance and clean exit.
- **Atlas Matter Steward** — ensures matter records, agent provenance, entitlements, transfer classification, and closure records are complete.

One person may hold multiple roles only where independence is not impaired. The Matter Principal and Value Office certifier may not be the same individual on an outcome-priced matter.

## 8. Promotion and appointment

Promotion is based on sustained evidence, not tenure alone.

Evaluation dimensions include:

- quality of judgment;
- client outcomes and durable transfer;
- professional integrity;
- evidence discipline;
- ability to operate under uncertainty;
- development of others;
- commercial judgment without sales distortion;
- contribution to methods and Atlas product learning;
- operating credibility where relevant;
- stewardship of confidentiality and client trust.

No one is promoted because they merely sold the most work, staffed the most people, or produced the most internal content.

## 9. Senior appointment gates

Appointment to Principal or above requires a documented panel decision. The panel evaluates at least three bodies of evidence:

1. **matter record** — what the candidate actually owned and how outcomes held up;
2. **judgment record** — examples of difficult calls, uncertainty, dissent, and correction;
3. **stewardship record** — client trust, people development, conflicts, confidentiality, economics, and institutional conduct.

A serious failure of integrity can block promotion regardless of commercial performance.

## 10. Governance forums

Advisory does not create committees merely to imitate a large partnership. It uses a small set of purpose-built forums:

### Executive Operating Review — monthly
President, management leaders, Finance, People & Culture, Risk/Legal as needed. Reviews P&L, pipeline quality, portfolio concentration, staffing, attrition, disputes, reserves, and major risks.

### Professional Quality Review — quarterly
Distinguished/Senior Principals and independent quality leadership. Reviews matter quality trends, review findings, agent incidents, transfer failures, client complaints, methodology, and lessons that should change practice.

### Carry Allocation Review — annual
President, Finance, People & Culture, Legal, and independent enterprise representative. Confirms eligibility, grants, vesting, valuation, forfeiture, and conflicts. No serving J2 person may be discussed as a prospective grantee.

### Reserved Matter Room — ad hoc
Temporary escalation mechanism for matters outside normal authority. It is dissolved when the decision is made and does not become a standing shadow committee.

## 11. Relationship ownership

Clients belong to the firm, not to individual rainmakers.

Relationship credit may affect compensation, but no individual may:

- restrict qualified colleagues from a client to preserve personal economics;
- suppress a conflict or quality concern to protect a relationship;
- promise a conclusion;
- promise carry, employment, or product concessions outside authority;
- keep material client context outside Atlas or the controlled relationship record.

## 12. Professional dissent

Professionals have a protected duty to challenge unsupported conclusions, unsafe interventions, manipulated outcome baselines, improper data use, or client pressure.

A reviewer may require unresolved dissent to appear in the matter record. The firm does not require artificial consensus where the evidence remains genuinely disputed.

## 13. Succession

No critical client relationship, methodology, product dependency, or professional authority may intentionally depend on one person. Senior professionals are expected to build successors, document methods, preserve decision records, and reduce key-person risk without diluting accountability.

## 14. Institutional test

Advisory should look and behave like a top professional institution even when it remains relatively small. The test is not headcount. The test is whether a sophisticated client, competitor, regulator, or prospective hire can see clear authority, professional standards, quality controls, economic discipline, and repeatable delivery without confusing bureaucracy for rigor.
