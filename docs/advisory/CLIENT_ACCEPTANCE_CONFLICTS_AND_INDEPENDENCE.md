# SABLE HARBOR ADVISORY — CLIENT ACCEPTANCE, CONFLICTS AND INDEPENDENCE

**Document ID:** `SH-ADV-006`  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Effective:** September 9, 2026  
**State:** `LOCKED DIRECTION`  
**Owner:** President, Sable Harbor Advisory with Legal/Risk  
**Related:** `SH-ADV-002`; enterprise conflicts, security and authority doctrine

## 1. Principle

Advisory does not accept every profitable matter. Client selection is a professional judgment about whether Sable Harbor can perform the work competently, independently, lawfully, securely and without compromising Sable Harbor operating businesses, J2, or another client.

The Acceptance Principal records the decision. Commercial enthusiasm is not acceptance authority.

## 2. Acceptance categories

Every proposed matter is classified before substantive work:

- **ACCEPT** — ordinary controls sufficient.
- **ACCEPT WITH CONDITIONS** — specific information barriers, scope restrictions, review, staffing, data restrictions, or contractual terms required.
- **HOLD** — facts, conflicts, authority, economics or data conditions unresolved.
- **DECLINE** — conflict, competence, integrity, authority, security, strategic or reputational risk cannot be responsibly mitigated.

## 3. Mandatory acceptance dimensions

The acceptance record must cover:

1. client identity and controlling interests;
2. decision owner and authority to engage Sable Harbor;
3. requested problem and whether the client is attempting to purchase a preferred conclusion;
4. direct and indirect conflicts;
5. competitor and operating-business conflicts;
6. J2 and restricted-information proximity;
7. legal/regulatory constraints;
8. competence and staffing;
9. Atlas/data rights, residency, retention and transfer boundaries;
10. outcome-fee independence and measurement feasibility;
11. public consequence, safety and operational risk;
12. reputational/integrity concerns;
13. sanctions, bribery, corruption, fraud or illicit-purpose indicators where applicable;
14. insurance/indemnity exposure;
15. ability to exit cleanly.

## 4. Competitor conflicts

Sable Harbor may advise competitors, but not by default.

A competitor matter is acceptable only when the firm can demonstrate that:

- the scope does not require use or disclosure of another client's confidential information;
- the work does not require Sable Harbor to exploit proprietary operating knowledge that belongs to one of its operating businesses rather than to general professional method;
- personnel can be staffed without creating unacceptable memory/confidentiality risk;
- Atlas entitlements and cross-matter learning controls can enforce separation;
- the client is informed of any conflict disclosure legally and contractually required;
- the firm can still exercise independent judgment.

## 5. Sable Harbor operating-business conflicts

Advisory may work for companies that compete with Pale Sun, ARU, Cradle, Foundry Field clients, or other Sable Harbor businesses, but the acceptance bar is higher.

The following are presumptively restricted:

- direct monetization of a current Sable Harbor operating-business trade secret;
- transfer of confidential unit economics, supplier terms, mine plans, logistics contracts, acquisition targets, security architecture or unpublished technical process details;
- advising a client in a live transaction directly adverse to a Sable Harbor acquisition, divestiture or financing position without explicit enterprise approval;
- using Advisory to evade an operating-business confidentiality or fiduciary boundary.

Generalized operating lessons, public facts, independently developed methods and client-specific work may be usable if Legal/Risk confirms the boundary.

## 6. J2 firewall

J2 is not a commercial information source.

Advisory may not accept a matter because a former J2 professional can provide privileged institutional knowledge, source identity, raw Collection, protected analysis, internal Orientation records, classified/restricted material, or nonpublic J2 relationships.

Former J2 professionals may use transferable professional skills, judgment habits, methods that have been lawfully released for enterprise use, and general experience. They may not commercialize protected J2 content.

Any matter whose commercial thesis depends on privileged J2 access is declined or redesigned.

## 7. Independence from client preference

The client may define the question, desired outcome, constraints and decision context. It may not purchase the conclusion.

Warning signs include:

- success fee contingent on a predetermined finding;
- scope written to exclude obviously relevant contrary evidence;
- request to remove uncertainty language;
- request to backfill a preferred number;
- demand that Sable Harbor represent advocacy as independent analysis;
- pressure to alter an Atlas evidence record or outcome baseline.

If the client wants advocacy, negotiation support or implementation assistance, the engagement may be structured honestly as such. It must not be mislabeled as independent judgment.

## 8. Outcome-fee conflicts

Outcome pricing creates an economic conflict and therefore requires controls.

At minimum:

- baseline and attribution are certified independently by the Value Office;
- the Matter Principal cannot unilaterally change payout metrics;
- fee calculations rely on agreed data sources;
- client obligations and externalities are explicit;
- material restatements are documented;
- professional conclusions do not change because a fee is at risk;
- matters where Sable Harbor would effectively grade its own subjective conclusion are fixed-fee Decision Matters, not outcome-fee matters.

## 9. Client concentration

Advisory monitors revenue and carry exposure by client, sector and relationship principal. No client should become so economically dominant that a rational professional would fear losing the firm by disagreeing with that client.

The Executive Operating Review may impose concentration caps, additional review or commercial diversification requirements.

## 10. Confidentiality barriers

Where conditions permit competitor or sensitive matters, the acceptance record may require:

- restricted matter team;
- separate Atlas workspace/tenant controls;
- no cross-matter agent memory;
- named access list;
- restricted method/productization review;
- clean-team procedures;
- source-specific handling rules;
- post-matter retention restrictions;
- independent reviewer from outside the conflicted commercial relationship.

Information barriers are controls, not magic. A matter is declined when the actual human or technical risk remains unreasonable.

## 11. Client integrity

Advisory may decline a client even when the requested work is technically legal if the relationship would require Sable Harbor to normalize material dishonesty, evidence destruction, bribery, deliberate safety evasion, sanctions circumvention, retaliation, or concealment of serious misconduct.

The firm is not obligated to remain engaged merely because a contract exists. Stop-work and termination rights should preserve the ability to leave.

## 12. Reacceptance

Long-duration matters and managed-continuity relationships are reaccepted at least annually and upon material change, including:

- acquisition or control change;
- new adverse party;
- scope expansion;
- new regulated activity;
- material litigation/investigation;
- new outcome-fee structure;
- significant security/data change;
- a Sable Harbor operating business entering the same competitive situation.

## 13. Conflict register

Atlas Meridian maintains the controlled matter conflict record, but Atlas does not make the acceptance decision autonomously. The system should support entity matching, relationship graphs, adverse-party checks, restrictions, waivers/consents where lawful, and reacceptance triggers.

## 14. Declination record

Material declined matters receive a concise record sufficient to show why the firm declined without retaining unnecessary sensitive information. Repeated client pressure for improper conclusions is an institutional signal and should be visible to future acceptance reviewers where lawful.

## 15. Test

A top-tier client should be able to understand why Sable Harbor can work for them while still believing that Sable Harbor will refuse work, protect competitors' information, protect its own crown jewels, and tell the client an unwelcome truth when the evidence requires it.
