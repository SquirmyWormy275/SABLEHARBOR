# SABLE HARBOR ADVISORY — KNOWLEDGE, METHOD AND CASE LEARNING STANDARD

## 1. Purpose

Advisory must become better because it has done work before without turning prior client material into an uncontrolled cross-client memory pool. Institutional learning therefore distinguishes professional method, generalized learning, matter-specific confidential information and restricted material.

## 2. Knowledge classes

### Firm method
Approved reusable professional methods, templates, evaluation frameworks, decision routines and quality controls owned/licensed by Sable Harbor.

### Generalized case learning
Lawfully abstracted lessons such as failure modes, causal patterns, implementation pitfalls, benchmark structures and professional heuristics that do not disclose client confidential content.

### Matter-specific client knowledge
Client data, facts, models, decisions, outputs and confidential context governed by the client's matter/tenant and contract.

### Restricted institutional knowledge
J2 records, source identities, protected operating-business secrets, privileged material and other information that cannot be commercialized or generalized merely because a professional encountered it.

## 3. After-action capture

Q2–Q4 matters complete a structured case-learning record containing, as permitted:

- original problem;
- what the team initially believed;
- what evidence changed the view;
- intervention/capability design;
- outcome;
- transfer result;
- what failed;
- unexpected effects;
- pricing/attribution lesson;
- model/agent lesson;
- reusable method opportunity;
- IP/confidentiality constraints.

The firm preserves being wrong. Failed hypotheses are institutional assets when accurately recorded.

## 4. Method ownership

Each controlled method has:

- owner;
- purpose;
- version;
- applicability;
- required evidence;
- known limitations;
- quality/review requirements;
- training prerequisites;
- Atlas/tool dependencies;
- last review date.

Methods are retired or superseded when evidence no longer supports them.

## 5. No template substitution

A prior deliverable is not automatically a method. Professionals may use prior structures, but must re-evaluate facts, assumptions, context and client authority. “Last year's deck” is not evidence.

## 6. Atlas cross-matter learning

Atlas Professional may support protected cross-matter learning only under controlled generalization. It must distinguish:

- client-specific matter memory;
- generalized firm method/lesson;
- source/restriction metadata;
- prohibited cross-client content.

Machine retrieval must enforce the same rights boundary expected of a human professional. A model is not permitted to leak client information through synthesis, comparison, embedding-neighbor behavior, graph shape or indirect inference.

## 7. Operating-business learning

Advisory may learn from Sable Harbor operating businesses, but transfer into professional method requires explicit classification. General lessons may be reusable; trade secrets and confidential unit-specific details remain protected.

When in doubt, the firm records the lesson at the highest safe level of abstraction rather than copying raw operating material into a general knowledge base.

## 8. J2 learning boundary

J2 skills and lawfully released methods may influence professional craft. Protected J2 matter content, raw Collection, Orientation records, source identities and restricted institutional memory do not become Advisory knowledge assets.

Former J2 professionals do not need to forget their professional formation, but they may not use protected content as a commercial shortcut.

## 9. Sanitized case library

Advisory maintains a sanitized case library for training and method development. Cases may be:

- client-approved named cases;
- properly de-identified real cases;
- composites where rights permit and no single client's secrets can be reconstructed;
- fully synthetic cases.

The case label states which type it is. Synthetic cases are never presented as real client credentials.

## 10. Benchmarking

Benchmarks must identify:

- population/source;
- time period;
- metric definition;
- comparability limitations;
- whether data is public, licensed, client-provided or synthetic;
- whether the benchmark is descriptive or normative.

Averages are not automatically best practice.

## 11. Publication review

External thought leadership derived from client or operating experience receives IP/confidentiality review. Professional conclusions must remain supportable and source state must be clear.

## 12. Method change governance

A material change to a firm method requires:

1. documented reason/evidence;
2. professional owner approval;
3. evaluation against representative cases;
4. Atlas/tool update where applicable;
5. release note/training impact;
6. retirement/supersession handling for prior version.

High-consequence methods receive independent challenge before broad adoption.

## 13. Search and discoverability

Knowledge should be searchable by problem, industry, operating system, method, evidence type, outcome, failure mode and expertise—not only by practice name. The common bench needs problem-centered retrieval.

## 14. Expert locator

The firm maintains a controlled expertise profile for professionals and approved external experts that distinguishes:

- demonstrated matter/operating experience;
- professional methods;
- languages/geographies;
- technical systems;
- review authority;
- conflicts/restrictions;
- current availability.

Expert profiles do not expose protected J2 assignments or client-confidential credentials.

## 15. Knowledge quality

Knowledge assets have owners and review dates. Stale material may remain historically accessible but should not silently appear as current guidance.

## 16. Institutional standard

A Tier 1 professional firm should not depend on individual memory, shared-drive folklore or uncontrolled generative retrieval. Sable Harbor's edge is accumulated institutional learning with provenance, rights and temporal integrity intact.
