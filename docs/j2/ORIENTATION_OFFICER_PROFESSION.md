## Vocation

Orientation Officer is a vocation, not an executive rotation, Chief-of-Staff credential, or stepping stone into power. The commissioned corps is fixed at approximately twenty-four billets: Head of Orientation, Deputy Head, four Senior Orientation Officers, and eighteen Orientation Officers. There are no junior or associate Orientation Officer grades. Entry into the commissioned corps occurs at Orientation Officer rank after selection and apprenticeship.

In exchange for exceptional compensation, unparalleled institutional access, intellectual freedom, research resources, sabbaticals, field exposure, education, and prestige, a commissioned officer permanently gives up later C-suite line office, operating VP authority, and equivalent line decision-making power. A serving Orientation Officer may not simultaneously hold a line executive role or voting board seat. Former officers may serve as non-executive directors only after a five-year cooling-off period and explicit disclosure of prior Orientation service. The profession does not become a backdoor route into executive power.

## Selection and admission

Selection is intentionally scarce and demanding. Orientation recruits from across the enterprise and from varied professional backgrounds rather than treating Judgment as the only feeder profession. A typical candidate will already have a substantial record of serious work and demonstrated judgment under pressure.

Candidates may nominate themselves and may also be nominated by others. Every application requires three recommendations: one from a person senior to the candidate, one from a peer, and one from a person junior to the candidate or otherwise meaningfully below the candidate in organizational status. There is no patronage lane; all applicants pass through the same process.

The initial packet includes background and records review, prior work review, writing samples, assigned written problems, and interviews. Candidates whose packets meet the threshold are invited to a five-to-seven-day in-person Assessment and Selection event at J2 Education.

### Assessment and Selection

A&S is deliberately opaque in event design while remaining bounded by professional safeguards. Candidates know the standards of conduct and that they are being assessed, but not which moments or exercises are dispositive. The environment uses ambiguity, incomplete information, status pressure, conflicting testimony, vague tasking, deliberate reversals, consequential uncertainty, and controlled fatigue to test calibration rather than theatrical endurance.

Each candidate is assigned the same Spartan residential room: bed, writing desk, secured workstation, and a red telephone. The phone has no ordinary social purpose and may ring at any hour with a task, recall, briefing request, or other event. The red phone is part of the shared professional lore of Orientation selection.

Education maintains mock decision environments including a boardroom, executive office, operations room, and interview rooms staffed by role players. Candidates may begin with written case material and later be summoned to brief a simulated board, interrogate incomplete evidence, revisit prior conclusions after new facts arrive, advise during a fast-moving catastrophe, or recognize that the responsible answer is to defer a noncritical decision until reliable judgment is possible.

Controlled sleep disruption is permitted as a diagnostic variable, not as punishment or an endurance contest. Some exercises occur late at night or after interrupted sleep specifically to test whether candidates recognize their own declining reliability, preserve calibration, communicate uncertainty, and resist unnecessary high-status urgency.

Blackridge-derived cases are part of the standing exercise library. Some are disguised so that candidates do not initially know their historical origin. Catastrophic scenarios may include industrial fatalities, major operational failures, capital crises, system breakdowns, or board-level uncertainty. The test is not whether a candidate can theatrically "solve" a disaster; it is whether the candidate can orient decision-makers to what is known, unknown, assumed, contested, and consequential.

Not every unusual event during A&S is a test. Education deliberately preserves ordinary, non-assessed moments so that the environment does not simply select for paranoia.

## Apprenticeship and commissioning

Passing A&S does not itself commission an Orientation Officer. Successful candidates enter an approximately one-year apprenticeship under experienced Orientation officers. The apprenticeship includes live work, supervised orientation, Education, field exposure, EIB work, and direct assessment of whether the candidate can carry the profession's access and independence without converting it into status or influence.

At the end of apprenticeship, an Admissions Council makes the final decision. Admission is not automatic, and deferral or non-selection is an ordinary outcome. Candidates may normally attempt the full selection process no more than twice in a career. Non-selection is not a disciplinary mark and no public ranking is issued.

Commissioning is marked by restrained ceremony. The candidate may be recalled to a room resembling the A&S environment and summoned by the red phone to report to the commissioning setting. A newly commissioned officer receives a numbered Orientation lapel pin. Because the corps is small, each pin is individually numbered and functions as a quiet professional symbol rather than corporate decoration.

## Tenure and circulation

Orientation is not a lifetime seat. The standard commission is six years. A commission may be extended, with eight years the normal maximum after renewal. Ten years is an absolute and rare cap requiring exceptional justification. Transition planning begins before the end of service so that turnover is orderly rather than abrupt.

Annual intake is small, normally on the order of one to three new commissioned officers depending on vacancies and apprenticeship outcomes. Rotation is deliberate so that the corps does not ossify into the same twenty-four people indefinitely.

Former officers may teach at Education, serve as fellows, conduct research, or contribute to the profession without exercising authority over sitting officers. Alumni status does not create a shadow chain of command.

## Work in decision environments

The officer knows the state, provenance, confidence, dissent, and limits of what Sable Harbor thinks it knows. In an executive or board room the officer has a narrow standing duty to intervene when material uncertainty is represented as fact, consequential dissent is omitted, confidence materially overstated, or a known information deficiency matters. The officer does not vote, direct the outcome, own the agenda, or automatically delay. “Noted and proceeding” is a valid response.

The Office of CEO and Board receive separate dedicated officers. The Board officer attends key board/committee meetings, supports the EIB, clarifies confidence/dissent/unknowns/judgment ownership, and may meet independent directors without management where appropriate. This is protection from exclusively management-filtered understanding, not a gotcha mechanism.

Finance is a standing observation post because beliefs become numbers people later mistake for facts. A senior officer works with the CFO office and approximately 2–3 embeds work across planning/performance areas as a refinable model. They sit with model builders, ask where assumptions came from and who owns them, connect them to Judgment Problems, inspect actual operations, and ask what variance taught about the belief beneath the number.

Temporary assignments follow consequential uncertainty: major acquisition, Red Wash expansion, transformative capital program, new regulated exposure, or similar environment. Presence is not a prestige perk.

## Anti-court architecture

Officers do not own operating deliverables, supervise line employees, control budgets, conduct personnel actions, gatekeep executive access or calendars, broker politics, map who is politically rising/falling, stockpile private intelligence, say “we” for an executive's business decision, or convert access into future executive power.

Material institutional information returns to the system through appropriate channels. Sable Harbor preserves judgments, dissent, assumptions, decisions, and knowledge changes; it deliberately does not build a forensic graph reconstructing which officer influenced which executive. Such a graph would be incomplete, scapegoating-prone, and itself politically toxic.

Executive-office assignments rotate approximately 24–36 months. Language names the office—“Orientation Officer assigned to the Office of the CEO”—not the person. Most officers are not permanently attached; they also work enterprise questions, EIB, Canon, research, field immersion, Education, and study tours.

## Professional self-government

Orientation is trusted to orient itself through a named Head/Director and a strong culture of professional challenge. There is no permanent steering committee above its thinking. The Head is first among rigor, not a czar of institutional truth.
