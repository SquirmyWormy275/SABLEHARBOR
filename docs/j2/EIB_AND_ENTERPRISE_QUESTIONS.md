The EIB is normally approximately two pages, enterprise-visible by default, a navigational front door rather than the house, and discussed rather than read aloud. Each category contains no more than five linked items: **What Sable Harbor Currently Believes; What Changed Our Mind; What Doesn't Fit; What We're Arguing About; What We Wish We Knew.** Items drill into Alexandria; evidence may remain restricted.

Sable Harbor maintains a short set of important enterprise questions, priority requirements, and plain-language “tell J2 if you see this” triggers. Every employee has the right and duty to contribute relevant signal. A submission is evidence, not institutional truth. Material signals receive documented disposition—no screaming into a void. The question stays visible whenever lawful even if relevant evidence is restricted.

## Form and editorial standard

The brief normally occupies approximately two pages. Each of its five categories contains no more than five items. Compression is disciplined selection, not removal of dissent or uncertainty. Each item states the point, why it matters now, confidence/state where material, and a route into deeper Alexandria material. Restricted evidence may remain restricted while the question and properly releasable institutional position remain visible.

**What Sable Harbor Currently Believes** contains consequential current beliefs or assumptions, not a corporate-news digest. **What Changed Our Mind** identifies the evidence or judgment that moved understanding and the prior state it superseded. **What Doesn't Fit** preserves anomalies that resist the current model. **What We're Arguing About** presents genuine competing interpretations and serious dissent. **What We Wish We Knew** exposes the highest-value unknowns and intelligence requirements.

Orientation owns selection, synthesis, dissent integrity, and publication. The briefer does not read the page aloud or convert it into a conclusion-delivery ritual. The EIB starts discussion, invites edge knowledge, and gives employees a navigational front door into institutional reasoning.

## Enterprise-wide access and sensing

The newest ARU hire should be able to see the same core questions as senior leadership unless the nature of information requires restriction. That employee may recognize a local observation leadership cannot see and becomes an institutional sensor. Submissions travel to Contact intake, retain raw signal, and receive a disposition through Judgment Watch or the relevant problem owner.

Priority questions and “tell J2 if you see this” triggers are written in plain language and specify useful observations without encouraging universal surveillance or accusation. A contributor does not become responsible for deciding whether the signal is true or enterprise-significant.

Every issued EIB remains in the Pinakes EIB Archive. Later correction or changed belief links to, rather than overwrites, the earlier brief so a historical reader can reconstruct the institutional state.
