# Sable Harbor — business-line development review

**Reviewed September 9, 2026. Repository baseline:** `SquirmyWormy275/SABLEHARBOR`, `main@aa0cf80467c25616ad04f6c76168db1eecaecbaa`. The local checkout matched the live GitHub branch. The tracked-file inventory contains 1,169 files, including 406 Markdown files. This assessment used the current authority index, complete v0.3.1 corporate-lore spine, later controlling closeouts, relevant operating and financial documents, selected implementation code, all 16 live open issues, and the live dispositions of PRs #9 and #13. This was a source and implementation review, not a fresh execution of every model, test, binary publication or historical artifact.

**Status:** Development assessment and proposed work packages. This report does not amend repository canon, implement the recommendations, or close issues. Financial amounts described below are properties of the repository's synthetic cases, not observed company results.

**The main finding:** Sable Harbor's development is uneven. Its industrial case already has detailed transactions, operating constraints, financial books, a five-year forecast and enterprise integration. Foundry Field, Atlas Meridian, Willow, Cradle and Advisory have established identities and useful software scaffolding, but their commercial, operational and financial populations need considerably more depth. The most valuable next work is to make each business's economics arise from its own activities and then connect those activities to consistent documents, records and enterprise reporting.

**3. Foundry Field: make the core software business commercially legible**

The repo establishes Foundry as the reusable substrate and Foundry Field as the deployable commercial product/service configuration. It also implements contract, performance-obligation, invoice, receipt and engagement objects. Existing reporting already includes ARR/customer and engagement interfaces. The opportunity is to deepen the source populations and their economic logic, rather than add duplicate reporting labels. [S12] [S13]

1. **Customer and contract lifecycle.** Build a dated synthetic customer/site register, product entitlement, implementation scope, term, renewal, expansion, contraction, termination and collection history. Connect subscriptions to service obligations and distinguish recurring platform revenue from implementation and continuing professional services. Derive ARR changes and customer concentration from that contract population.
2. **Customer economics.** Build cohort and customer contribution models incorporating deployment labor, support burden, hosting, vendor licenses, credits, customer-success effort and unusual maintenance obligations. Add gross/net revenue retention and acquisition-cost/payback views only when the underlying selling-cost and cohort records exist. Define metrics before calculating them.
3. **Delivery and support evidence.** Add discovery notes, mapping specifications, source-authority matrices, implementation acceptance, customer training, support tickets, service credits and renewal reviews. A strong reference dossier would follow one customer from initial scope through a successful deployment, an ordinary exception and a later renewal.
4. **Product and configuration lifecycle.** Give local adaptations dispositions: ordinary configuration, supported extension, product improvement, managed exception, retirement or unresolved question. Connect these to release/change records and the recurring cost of maintaining them. Use the Crossing's customization scars without making every exception a defect.
5. **Shared-substrate accounting.** Distinguish platform R&D, delivery cost and shared Foundry capability used by Atlas or internal operating companies. Internal consumption must not produce fictional external revenue or cause the same engineering cost to appear in several business P&Ls.

**Can proceed as implementation:** model/schema design, documented assumptions, synthetic cohorts, dossier templates and reconciliation logic. **Canon-dependent:** exact permanent customer identities, final application names, leadership/title choices and accepted commercial tariffs where the repo still leaves them open.

**4. Atlas Meridian: connect investigative performance to commercial viability**

Atlas has a substantial origin story: Hound, Ranger, the historical 25-case gauntlet, the early-2026 capability threshold and Simone Vale's bridge program. Its current financial flow records a model version, question, compute cost, validation cost and customer fee. That is useful scaffolding but does not yet capture the full business of delivering repeatable investigations. The historical approximately nine-of-25 result is a dated milestone, not a current performance claim. [S07] [S14]

1. **Design-partner and investigation register.** Give each prospective or synthetic partner a scoped question, evidence boundary, permitted tools, data readiness, responsible reviewer, stop conditions, delivery criteria and commercial stage. Distinguish research trials, paid pilots, repeat engagements and generally available product commitments.
2. **Evaluation casebooks.** Implement public, versioned examples showing source retrieval, competing interpretations, abandoned branches, missing authority, unsupported answers, human validation and later corrections. Link model/version changes to comparable evaluation runs. Private assessment material stays in its existing separate boundary.
3. **Per-investigation economics.** Calculate ingestion/preparation effort, model/tool consumption, compute, reviewer time, field validation, reruns, support, security requirements and elapsed delivery capacity. Model price and margin against that workload. A plausible answer should not automatically count as a successful paid investigation.
4. **Bridge-to-product gates.** Build explicit readiness records for repeatability, evidence quality, entitlement behavior, support ownership and deployment qualification. Track open work and cost-to-complete against the twelve-month bridge, without manufacturing a commercial launch date.
5. **Customer deliverables and rights.** Draft the investigation brief, engagement assumptions, evidence report, correction process and customer handoff. Identify client data rights, result reuse, confidentiality and how Foundry Field dependencies affect packaging.

**Can proceed:** all supporting records and reversible economic scenarios. **Canon-dependent:** permanent design-partner names, final runtime/vendor choices, launch commitments, approved pricing and ownership changes. Atlas remains decision support; it does not receive acquisition, capital or operating authority.

**5. Willow: turn the existing finance proposal into populated operational records**

It explicitly keeps Willow a parent-company laboratory/cost center and Klein a closed historical program. Its 24-person Willow population is a working financial calibration, not a newly locked establishment. [S05]

1. **Project portfolio and budget.** Populate experiments with question, sponsor, budget, commitments, purchases, labor/compute consumption, observation and continue/change/transfer/kill decision. Preserve worthwhile failed experiments and distinguish them from control failures.
2. **Fort site and fixed-asset register.** Populate Big Shed research equipment, Small Shed office/temporary lodging, White Shed intake/storage and site utilities. Track purchase/lease assumptions, maintenance, calibration, depreciation, retirement and retained Museum artifacts. Keep exact unsupported property details open.
3. **Klein-to-Willow transition schedule.** Record the disposition of historical equipment, customer commitments, prototypes and methods: transferred, retired, scrapped, retained or unresolved. Treat the 2022 change as an internal program transition, not an acquisition that creates goodwill.
4. **Qualification and production transfer.** Connect a successful experiment to a receiving operating/product owner, test evidence, operating envelope, maintenance responsibility, transfer cost, acceptance and later performance. A prototype does not become a supported production service by continued use alone.
5. **Historical financial/control evidence.** Develop the supported Sar-e-Sang travel/expense reconstruction and other dated experiment records, maintaining the canon's uncertainty. Do not turn unsupported spending into a proven criminal payment or invent official findings.

**Best result:** a monthly Willow management pack showing spending, commitments, assets and decisions, plus a traceable transfer into a receiving business. No artificial utilization target or internal-revenue quota is needed.

**6. Cradle: replace allocation-driven economics with the accepted physical chain**

The current recovery function already protects host ownership and calculates recovered units, a sale and host participation. However, the standard enterprise generator fixes feed at 100 tons, grade at 0.001 and recovery at 0.80, then derives the selling price from a target revenue allocation. The September 6 closeout explicitly calls for the successor to derive economics from business drivers instead. This is a direct, source-supported implementation gap. [S03] [S15]

1. **Stream 17 reference model.** Model eligible material, composition, recoverability, availability, captured product, assay/payability, actual modeled accepted sales, host participation, equipment cost, maintenance and settlement timing. Keep the established mixed-concentrate product and host-authority boundary.
2. **Demotte Gen 1 operating model.** Use water/slipstream flow, time-varying chemistry, capture performance, fouling, cleaning intervals, consumables, maintenance and bypass periods. Do not force the AMD process into the same feed-tonnage model as a mineral separation stream.
3. **Bedford batch and upgrading model.** Track receipts, genealogy acceptance, storage, batch combination, process yields, rejects/residues, upgrading/blending costs and outbound specialist-refiner acceptance. Preserve unit conversions and separate captured material from saleable, accepted product.
4. **Commercial documentation.** Build recovery-right, capture/title-point, equipment, maintenance, bypass/stop, downstream sales, assay-adjustment, host-settlement and termination schedules. Model economic terms as explicit assumptions where the closeout deliberately avoids fixing permanent numbers.
5. **Deployment portfolio economics.** Build prospect screening, sampling, technical kill gates, deployment capital, commissioning, replacement and cash recovery. Include abandoned prospects and Gen 1-to-Gen 2 learning. Do not require every target to become a project or every scenario to be profitable.
6. **Enterprise replacement bridge.** Retire the relevant generic Cradle allocation in the new model and add the new bottom-up records exactly once. Reconcile quantities, inventory, direct costs, host obligations, receivables and cash to enterprise books.

The locked genealogy is the acceptance spine: source → host process → designated stream → sample/assay → recovery run → captured intermediate → Bedford batch → outbound product → downstream acceptance → host settlement → financial record. The settled Cradle concept does not require another owner decision before this implementation can be prepared.

5. **Industrial depth where evidence changes a decision.** Build the standalone mine-upgrade case, granular logistics service economics and closure/funding sensitivities on the existing v2 platform.
6. **Full unit packages, local controls and current publications.** Complete #12 incrementally by unit, then reconcile the enterprise and institutional interfaces. Publication work should accompany completed source packages rather than accumulate indefinitely at the end.

For each substantive package, completion should mean: a source-linked current business dossier; explicit assumptions and unresolved facts; modeled operational records that drive the economics; reconciled subledgers/journals/statements; applicable local controls and naturally produced evidence; correctly scoped unit exports; synchronized required publications and structured records; validation appropriate to the change; and an accepted repository delivery record. Preparing a model does not itself lock its scenario inputs as canon.

**Practical judgment:** The strongest immediate combination is Foundry Field/Advisory commercial depth, Cradle's bottom-up successor and Willow's populated finance model, supported by one consistent workforce/cost/financial bridge. The industrial side provides a useful quality benchmark and integration base. Most implementation can be prepared from existing canon and transparent scenario assumptions; the relatively few permanent naming, authority, pricing, legal-policy and runtime choices can be isolated into concrete decision packets.
