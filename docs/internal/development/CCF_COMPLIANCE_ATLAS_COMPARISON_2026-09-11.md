# CCF preparation compared with Compliance Atlas

**Review date:** September 11, 2026. **State:** Comparative development assessment; no imported framework requirements, new compliance commitment or change to corporate authority.

**Compared revisions:** Sable Harbor CCF preparation `1766b20` ([PR #125](https://github.com/SquirmyWormy275/SABLEHARBOR/pull/125)); Compliance Atlas [`4a6e1e8b1bb0e4111d11630e80e888a648d45aed`](https://github.com/shashankvaidya99/compliance-atlas/tree/4a6e1e8b1bb0e4111d11630e80e888a648d45aed).

## Conclusion

The preparation's architecture is compatible with the most useful Atlas ideas: native control identity, scoped evidence populations, distinct tests and conclusions, and separation between implementation and assurance. The comparison does not justify rebuilding the register. It identifies the next layers to add: assessment planning, evidence reuse, framework-specific applicability and presentation, and a stronger versioned source catalog.

Compliance Atlas is an editorial HTML reference, not an executable GRC backend. The inspected tree contains a README, MIT license, fourteen HTML files, and no database migrations, authenticated evidence workflow, source connectors, tests or package dependency/build manifest. Twelve HTML volumes cover frameworks; two supply entry/directory pages. The README still advertises eleven volumes. The volume scripts switch presentation panels; they do not execute the described audit procedures. [Pinned repository tree](https://github.com/shashankvaidya99/compliance-atlas/tree/4a6e1e8b1bb0e4111d11630e80e888a648d45aed), [SOC 2 source](https://github.com/shashankvaidya99/compliance-atlas/blob/4a6e1e8b1bb0e4111d11630e80e888a648d45aed/atlas_v02_soc2.html).

I inspected the file inventory and twelve volumes' structure/content, with closer review of evidence, crosswalk, AI and assurance passages. Selected high-impact claims were checked against the originating publishers. This is not a complete validation of every claim, criterion, cost estimate, date or mapping in the Atlas.

## Material guidance that should not be imported as a rule

**Ownership.** The SOX volume assigns program ownership to Internal Audit. Sable Harbor's accepted doctrine makes management responsible for controls and remediation while IA evaluates independently. That boundary remains controlling. PCAOB AS 2201 paragraph .85D likewise distinguishes management's responsibility for ICFR and its assessment from the auditor's opinion. Internal coordination does not transfer management accountability to IA. [Atlas SOX volume](https://github.com/shashankvaidya99/compliance-atlas/blob/4a6e1e8b1bb0e4111d11630e80e888a648d45aed/atlas_v01_sox.html), [PCAOB AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201).

**Sampling and reliance.** Do not hard-code the Atlas's frequency/sample-size table, uplift percentages or blanket selection prohibitions. PCAOB AS 2315 .38–.39 ties control sampling to deviation tolerance, expected deviations, risk and representative selection; .24 explicitly recognizes haphazard selection for substantive samples. AS 2201 .19 makes reliance on others' work risk-sensitive rather than supplying a universal prohibition for an entire named control family. A future assessment record needs scope, objective, methodology, rationale and assessor approval. [PCAOB AS 2315](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2315), [AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201).

**FedRAMP pathway freshness.** The May-2026-labeled volume still presents JAB-versus-agency selection as a current planning fork. GSA announced the Board replacing the JAB in May 2024, and FedRAMP's August 2024 transition notice described moving away from authorization tiers based on those paths. That is enough to require refreshed primary-source review before using its lifecycle as a current implementation plan. This comparison does not attempt to restate the entire current program. [GSA announcement](https://www.gsa.gov/about-gsa/newsroom/news-releases/fedramp-board-launched-to-support-safe-secure-use-05142024), [FedRAMP transition notice](https://www.fedramp.gov/archive/2024-08-12-moving-to-one-fedramp-authorization-an-update-on-the-jab-transition/).

**AIUC-1 version reconciliation.** The Atlas volume describes 51 active controls. The publisher's current scoping page describes 50 requirements and capability-dependent scoping. Counts and terminology may reflect differing editions or units; do not treat either number as a timeless schema invariant or blindly import the Atlas crosswalk. [AIUC-1 scoping](https://www.aiuc-1.com/scoping). The publisher also distinguishes certification from a guarantee of future safety; the existing NOT_ASSERTED boundary remains appropriate. [AIUC-1 FAQ](https://www.aiuc-1.com/faq).

**Source authority.** Volumes name reference families and verification dates, but the inspected volume files provide no external citation hyperlinks. Some references are plain text. The Atlas is useful for finding subjects and visual explanations; its verification labels are not our source review. Pin publisher, exact edition/requirement, retrieval date, access/license constraints, effective interval and reviewer before a mapping is accepted. No Atlas code or artwork is incorporated by this comparison.

## Additions to the next build tranche

1. **Source and framework catalog.** Record external source identity/version, publisher URL, effective/retrieved dates, access state, reviewer and applicability status. Add HIPAA, EU AI Act and AIUC-1 as explicit research entries where they are absent from the existing source-family plan. No automatic adoption.
2. **Assessment contract.** Add assessment ID/type, scoped criteria and boundary, period, control version, test objectives/attributes, selection method/rationale, required evidence, authorized reviewer and conclusion. Preserve native management monitoring separately from external practitioner requirements.
3. **Evidence reuse graph.** Let one immutable artifact support several tests through separate references, scope checks and conclusions. Add source-report accuracy/completeness review and reject reuse when period, population, transformation, rights or tested assertion differs.
4. **Applicability and management-system views.** Generate a native applicability review plus framework-specific SoA/profile views from explicit mappings. Track inclusions, exclusions, justification, treatment, owner and approval. Never label the current 1,660 pending rows a completed external-framework SoA.
5. **AI-specific implementation records.** Inventory systems, models, datasets, capabilities, tools, rights, human decisions and material changes. Link adversarial and operational evaluations to native controls. Keep private evaluator material outside the public archive.
6. **Finding aggregation and dependency impact.** Link a failed access/change/reporting dependency to affected assessments, then assess the consequence. Do not automatically declare every dependent control failed or compensate with an arbitrary sample multiplier.
7. **Read-only visual explorer.** Generate domain/control, lifecycle, evidence, gap and mapping views from the same register. Keep source/period/status/limitations visible and avoid a misleading aggregate compliance score.

These are proposed additions to the existing audit backlog, primarily CCF-05 through CCF-14. The comparison does not change the preparation's acceptance criteria or authorize mandatory external commitments. The completed register and three examples remain the correct foundation for this work.
