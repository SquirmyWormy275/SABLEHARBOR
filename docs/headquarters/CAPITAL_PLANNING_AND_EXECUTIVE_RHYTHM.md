# CAPITAL, PLANNING, PERFORMANCE, AND EXECUTIVE RHYTHM

## Finance doctrine

Finance is **imperial over financial truth, bounded on business judgment**. The CFO owns the integrity of the enterprise's economic record, common financial language, treasury and capital discipline, and the ability to state the economics plainly. Finance does not acquire the right to run a business by proxy simply because economics are measurable.

Financial truth is one input to management judgment, not the whole of it. This boundary is an explicit control against a finance ministry that optimizes only what it can measure.

## Capital allocation rhythm

Sable Harbor uses continuous capital allocation with nested clocks:

- **Continuous:** businesses operate with real authority inside standing capital envelopes. Extraordinary opportunities may be raised whenever they arise; no artificial calendar gate prevents a good decision.
- **Quarterly:** each business refreshes its forward capital outlook and material opportunity set in a concise form.
- **Annual:** corporate compares opportunities across the portfolio and reallocates incremental capital according to durable value, not last year's budget.
- **Ad hoc:** transformative or time-sensitive opportunities are evaluated when they exist.

Businesses control operations and local capital inside their authority. Corporate controls surplus/incremental capital and decides where the next enterprise dollar goes regardless of which business generated the cash.

There is no use-it-or-lose-it budgeting. Returning unused capital is evidence of judgment, not failure. Capital follows opportunity, not politics, history, or organizational size.

## Planning and performance

Forecasts are estimates, not promises. The annual plan coordinates the enterprise; it is not an evaluation trap.

The rhythm is:

- **Continuous visibility:** shared systems provide current financial and operating information without recurring requests for presentation theater.
- **Exception-triggered dialogue:** material change travels quickly; bad news is not held for the next calendar meeting.
- **Quarterly business review:** a real conversation among the CEO, CFO, business president, and only the people germane to the issues. Common lenses include economics, market, operations, people, and capital. There is no mandatory slide walk.
- **Annual strategy and capital review:** deep examination of strategy and capital rather than a ceremonial budget negotiation.

Common measures are strongest at the economic level. Operating measures remain business-specific where the businesses are genuinely different.

Sable Harbor does not punish honest forecasting error merely because reality differed. It evaluates actual results, decision quality, candor, and what was reasonably knowable at the time. This separation is intended to reduce sandbagging and encourage early signal.

## Decision doctrine

For each consequential decision, name one accountable owner and make explicit the decision, authority, timing, material inputs, and what would trigger reconsideration. Consultation can be broad; accountability remains singular. No one owns a decision merely by being in the room.

Challenge is expected before decision. Material dissent is preserved. After a decision, ambiguity about who owns execution is dysfunction.

Documentation is triggered by stakes, not habit. Routine decisions require little or no formal record. Consequential decisions require enough record to preserve owner, evidence, serious alternatives, material dissent, reasoning, and reconsideration conditions. The most consequential/existential matters receive proportionately deeper challenge. Autonomy does not require paperwork merely to prove autonomy; restrictions on autonomy carry the burden of justification.

Sable Harbor trains judgment as a core competency. Decisions are reviewed against what could reasonably have been known at the time, not solely against eventual outcome.

## Meetings

Meetings are expensive in direct time and cognitive interruption. The default is no meeting when a note, direct conversation, or asynchronous decision can accomplish the work.

A meeting should be able to name:

- the decision or purpose;
- the accountable owner;
- why each participant is needed.

If it cannot, it should not convene. Sable Harbor does not create paperwork or standing meetings to prove that autonomy exists.

## Executive architecture: three concentric rings

Sable Harbor separates decision authority from information breadth.

### 1. Authority core

A very small inner group exists for enterprise decisions requiring speed and reserved corporate authority. Its size is driven by actual authority, not representation. The exact permanent seat list remains refinable rather than being invented for symmetry.

### 2. Enterprise staff / information ring

A broad middle ring - roughly 25-40 people as a working scale - includes business presidents and key functional leaders because information should not be forced through narrow management chains. Membership follows information value and changes as the enterprise changes. The group is problem-driven, not a round-robin status cabinet.

### 3. Temporary issue rooms

For consequential problems, one owner convenes every person whose direct knowledge materially helps. The room exists for the issue and dissolves when the issue does. It does not become a standing committee.

J2 supports information fidelity across all three rings. It forms common factual pictures, identifies what changed, assumptions, uncertainty, and dissent; it does not inherit the decision.

Default information posture is broad internal visibility subject to restriction by the nature of information rather than rank. Authority-core members receive decision-ready synthesis, the enterprise staff receives broad context, and issue rooms receive the depth required for the problem.
