# ENTERPRISE SUPPORT SERVICES CHARTER

**Document ID:** `SH-HQ-ESS-001` | **Version:** 1.0.0 | **Effective:** September 3, 2026 | **State:** LOCKED
**Owner:** Sable Harbor Headquarters | **Related:** Office of the CEO, Finance, OGC, Risk & Compliance, People & Culture, Technology Services, Internal Audit, J2

## Purpose

Enterprise Support Services (ESS) is Sable Harbor's administrative umbrella for the enterprise functions that support the operating businesses. ESS exists to make the businesses stronger, reduce friction, preserve institutional capability, and provide coherent shared foundations without becoming a shadow operating company.

Membership in ESS does **not** create a substantive reporting chain. The Chief of ESS is an administrative head, not a super-executive, COO-by-another-name, or substantive superior to the CFO, General Counsel, technology leadership, People & Culture leadership, or other corporate officers. Those officers retain direct substantive standing with the CEO and, where appropriate, the Board.

## ESS functions

The headquarters functions placed within ESS are:

- Finance;
- Office of the General Counsel, including the Corporate Secretary function;
- Risk & Compliance;
- People & Culture;
- Technology Services / enterprise IT;
- enterprise security capability;
- procurement and supply support;
- safety/environmental governance where exercised at enterprise level;
- quality and technical standards where exercised at enterprise level;
- facilities and ordinary administrative services.

The list describes the administrative home. It does not imply that ESS owns operating decisions that belong to a business, site, product, or accountable executive.

## Functions outside ESS

- The Board remains a governance body, not an ESS function.
- The Office of the CEO remains the executive center.
- J2 remains institutionally separate.
- Internal Audit remains completely separate in function and authority, with functional accountability to the Board Audit & Compliance Committee and only administrative support relationships as required.
- Operating businesses remain outside ESS and own their operations and results.

## Office of the General Counsel

OGC is deliberately traditional and bounded. Legal advises; management decides. Legal has authority where the law itself creates a hard boundary, but it does not substitute legal preference for management judgment. OGC is expected to find lawful paths rather than merely block activity. Privilege is protected but is not a hiding mechanism.

The Corporate Secretary sits within OGC and coordinates board mechanics, entity governance, corporate records, and enterprise continuity/crisis-governance playbooks. Resilience is a standing coordination duty rather than a separate bureaucracy: OGC/Corporate Secretary coordinates the governance mechanism while Finance, Technology Services, People & Culture, security, and operating businesses execute within their domains.

## Risk & Compliance

Risk and Compliance are one management function. It maintains workable risk and compliance architecture, policy governance, risk visibility, common-control stewardship, and escalation when exposure exceeds accepted boundaries. Management owns risk. Compliance operationalizes requirements. Legal interprets law. Internal Audit independently evaluates. J2 is not an audit or compliance function.

The function is explicitly anti-performative: controls should be designed around the work, effort follows exposure, and processes that exist only to demonstrate compliance should be challenged.

## Administrative doctrine

ESS centralizes support **without centralizing judgment**. It exists to provide capability, coherence, and service quality. Corporate support functions may own professional standards, common platforms, and enterprise obligations; they do not gain operating authority merely because they sit at headquarters.
