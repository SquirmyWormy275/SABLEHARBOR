# PEOPLE & CULTURE DOCTRINE

**Document ID:** `SH-HQ-PC-001` | **Version:** 1.0.0 | **Effective:** September 3, 2026 | **State:** LOCKED DIRECTION
**Owner:** People & Culture | **Related:** Education, ESS, business leadership, compensation and human capital governance

## Institutional purpose

People & Culture exists to make Sable Harbor an exceptional place for exceptional people to do serious work over a long career. **Continuity is capital.** Experience, relationships, craft, and institutional memory compound; routine churn destroys capability that cannot be recreated by hiring volume.

Sable Harbor is not an up-or-out institution. Technical and professional depth are first-class destinations, and exceptional specialists may out-earn managers. Management is a separate profession, not the mandatory reward for competence.

## The boring functions: invisible excellence

Payroll, benefits, leave, accommodations, employee records, required training administration, employee relations, investigations when required, and separations are treated as operational disciplines. Their design rule is **invisible excellence**:

- one front door where practical;
- self-service where it truly removes friction, never as a deflection shield;
- recurring employee pain is treated as a product defect;
- the default is to do less, not add process;
- mandatory processes have a purpose, an owner, and should disappear when the requirement disappears;
- a benefit is evaluated by what reaches the employee after money, time, hassle, stigma, and other friction.

P&C has a standing duty to hunt organizational "own goals": benefits cancelled out by parking costs, policies that consume the value they claim to create, or administrative burdens felt most heavily by junior employees. Where the company requires attendance at a workplace, it should not make employees pay unnecessary costs simply to comply; practical commuting support, including free on-premise parking where applicable and useful transit support, is part of the design.

## Mandatory learning

P&C administers the mechanism for mandatory training so it cannot hide elsewhere. The requiring function owns the reason and substantive requirement; Education owns pedagogy and instructional quality; P&C owns coherent delivery and employee experience. Mandatory never means intentionally lifeless.

## Talent philosophy

Recruiting seeks four broad kinds of talent: proven masters, high-slope early-career people, unconventional excellence that conventional resumes miss, and people who know something Sable Harbor does not. The standing scouting question is: **Who would we regret never meeting?**

Talent acquisition is demanding but dignified. The side door is a right to be seen, not a privileged hiring standard. Scouts, employee nominations, and open applicants converge into the same evidence-based selection system after the initial screen.

Once Sable Harbor asks a candidate for human time beyond the initial application screen, Sable Harbor owes that candidate a human conclusion. Candidates who have entered substantive human interaction receive prompt, specific, respectful feedback if they are not selected.

## Talent scout profession and rotation

Talent scouting is a prestigious rotational assignment open to strong practitioners inside or outside P&C. It is not a requisition-processing desk. People preparing for management receive talent judgment instruction; those who demonstrate aptitude or interest can pursue deeper scout qualification. A standard scout tour is approximately 12 months, with a possible six-month extension.

The talent cycle is:

**Spot -> Assess -> Evaluate -> Recruit -> Develop -> Run.**

Everyone with serious people responsibility is expected to understand the cycle. Not everyone becomes a scout, but hiring and development are not outsourced morally to HR.

## Candidate selection flow

After initial screening or a scout/employee nomination, the common flow is:

1. A 45-60 minute domain-aware human conversation focused on what the candidate has done, what interests them, and whether both sides want to invest more time.
2. A 90-120 minute work encounter resembling real work, conducted collaboratively rather than as take-home theater.
3. One standardized exploration assessment selected to answer the most important remaining question about the candidate.
4. References where useful, aimed at understanding placement, complementarity, and the conditions in which the candidate is excellent.
5. A small selection conference with independent written observations. The hiring manager owns the decision.
6. A fast personal offer or a candid no.

### Standardized exploration assessment menu

The eight locked assessments are:

**1. The Unknown.** The candidate receives a fixed case file about a fictional research-station systems failure. They receive documents, logs, diagrams, weather, and emails; they may ask a limited number of scripted questions; the file then closes. They brief what happened, what supports the conclusion, what remains unknown, and three actions for the next 24 hours.

**2. The Build.** The candidate receives a standardized physical kit and must bridge a fixed gap to carry a stated load. Midway through the exercise the payload changes. The structure is tested, followed by a debrief on choices, failures, and the next design.

**3. The Teach.** The candidate brings one non-job topic they genuinely know and care about, with no slide deck and only one object or page. They teach two assessors; assessors ask standardized questions about misconception, serious disagreement, and the fastest path to competence, then deliberately misstate the lesson and ask the candidate to repair the misunderstanding.

**4. The Hand-off.** The candidate receives a fixed project file with timelines, budget, emails, and contradictions. They then meet a scripted outgoing project owner who possesses additional facts. After the handoff, the candidate briefs a second assessor on what was inherited, what matters, and what happens next.

**5. Judgment Under Trade-offs.** The candidate acts as the head of a fictional business facing a degrading critical machine and chooses among three viable options with explicit costs and probabilities. New information is then introduced and the candidate may retain or change the decision.

**6. Critique.** The candidate reviews a polished acquisition recommendation containing real strengths and planted flaws, then meets the recommendation's scripted author and ultimately advises whether to proceed, modify, or stop, including what should happen next.

**7. Prioritization.** The candidate receives a fixed Monday-morning board of sixteen issues spanning customer, safety, regulatory, financial, and team matters. They sort the work into do now, delegate, schedule, or not today. A standardized new event arrives mid-exercise and the candidate must adjust and defend the ordering.

**8. Self-correction.** The candidate makes a decision on a fixed market-entry case, submits it, receives a new evidence packet, then issues a second decision and explains what changed, why, and what evidence could change the conclusion again.

The menu is standardized in packet, timing, and prompt. It is not a personality game. The chosen assessment is explained to the candidate as the question Sable Harbor still wants to understand.

## Offers and compensation

Sable Harbor offers what it believes is a fair number if the candidate says yes immediately. Negotiation skill is not a compensation competency. If new information shows the company's initial number was wrong, the company corrects it; otherwise the fair number stands. Salary-history games are prohibited.

Base pay is strong and proactive. Compensation supports continuity rather than bargaining. Technical and professional ladders can exceed managerial compensation. Counteroffers should be rare because compensation health is reviewed before an employee must threaten departure.

Broad-based economic participation is a design principle: fair salary for current work, meaningful profit sharing in genuinely strong years, and long-duration employee ownership that compounds with continuity. A conservatively structured ESOP or similar trust is favored in spirit, without using crushing leverage simply to create employee ownership. Governance remains with the Board and management; economic participation does not create an accidental co-op governance structure.

## Performance and growth

Performance review is deliberately simple and candid. The core manager conversation is built around two questions:

- How is the work going?
- Where could you go from here?

The first is about present work, candor, expectations, and evidence. The second is a separate **Next Chapter** conversation about curiosity, latent strengths, process improvement, mobility, and what the company could enable. Pay decisions draw from evidence of work, not from performance-review theater.

Employees may explore internal opportunities without manager permission. Current managers are consulted but do not own a veto. Talent hoarding is treated as a management failure. Every employee should have a P&C talent partner or other route outside the direct management chain when the manager relationship is a poor fit.

Team chemistry is real institutional value. Sable Harbor may preserve combinations of people who are unusually effective together, but teams must remain permeable: internal mobility, transparent opportunities, new inflow, and no clique immunity.

## Managers

Managers are selected and formed, not simply promoted. Managerial training includes labor and employment mechanics, compensation, mobility, hiring, the talent cycle, difficult conversations, and practical cases. Early manager appointments are supported by a P&C partner and outside perspective. Returning to a professional track is honorable and does not erase professional standing.

## Succession

Succession is capital preservation, not a secret high-potential aristocracy. Critical roles are examined in terms of immediate coverage, readiness now, and development over time. People are developed for broader institutional responsibility rather than anointed as the single successor. A critical role that cannot be handed over is a design failure.

## Workforce planning and reductions

Hiring is a long-duration capital decision. Businesses maintain enough forward visibility to identify growing, stable, and potentially surplus capability before a crisis. Before reductions, Sable Harbor favors redeployment, reskilling, internal transfer, temporary bridging where real work exists, and pauses external hiring for equivalent capability. A continuity reserve may fund bridging, relocation, or retraining so individual businesses are not punished for preserving enterprise capability.

Sable Harbor does **not** use layoffs simply to polish profitability while useful human capability and healthy economics remain. Automation creates capacity before it creates a case for casualties. If a reduction becomes genuinely unavoidable, the company exhausts reasonable reversible levers first, applies leadership sacrifice before ordinary-worker pain where appropriate, selects against disappearing work rather than convenient people, communicates plainly, provides meaningful severance and benefit continuation, treats vesting seriously, and supports actual placement. A reduction in force is a management necessity or failure condition, not a moral indictment of affected employees.

If leadership would be unwilling to explain the condition, sequence of levers, and reasoning openly to the people bearing the consequence, the decision is not ready.

## Benefits and life

Benefits are designed around a long career rather than maximum brochure value. The company provides strong health coverage, meaningful retirement support, substantial parental and life-event leave, actual time off, and long-tenure sabbaticals. Well-being is not wellness surveillance. The first question is whether the work itself is broken.

Hybrid work follows a simple rule: **work where the work works best.** Solitary work can be done from home where appropriate. Teams come together when collaboration, mentorship, or the nature of the work materially benefits. Teams establish useful rhythms rather than arbitrary attendance quotas. The office must earn the commute through good spaces, useful collaboration, real culture, and friction-free basics rather than forced fun.

## Listening and dignity

Employee listening is human, voluntary, and anti-panopticon: stay conversations, exit conversations, small groups, P&C presence, and direct channels. P&C does not build passive behavioral surveillance in the name of engagement.

Employee relations keeps ordinary human problems ordinary where possible: coaching, mediation, movement, or a direct conversation before formal case machinery. Formal investigations are used when warranted. No surprises, no silent files, and dignified separations are the standard.
