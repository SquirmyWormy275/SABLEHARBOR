# ENTERPRISE AUTHORITY, PORTFOLIO, AND PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE

**Document ID:** `SH-HQ-AUTH-002` | **Version:** 1.0.0 | **Effective:** September 3, 2026 | **State:** LOCKED DIRECTION
**Owner:** Office of the CEO | **Related:** Governance Constitution, ESS, business presidents, J2, professional functions

## Architecture

Sable Harbor uses **vertical authority, horizontal expertise, and shared foundations**. It is deliberately not a dual-accountability matrix.

Authority is mapped to decisions rather than dotted-line reporting. The practical layers are Board, corporate center, business, operating unit, and individual. Authority is assumed local unless explicitly reserved.

- The Board owns governance, CEO selection, capital structure, and reserved/existential matters.
- The corporate center owns institutional integrity, portfolio capital allocation, shared foundations, and explicitly reserved enterprise standards.
- Businesses own strategy and execution within their envelopes.
- Operating units own real local operating decisions.
- Individuals exercise judgment inside delegated authority.

Dotted lines do not decide.

## Professional practices

Functions own the profession; businesses own the work. Corporate professional functions receive three kinds of authority where applicable:

1. **Standards authority** - define enterprise professional requirements.
2. **Professional authority** - develop people, qualifications, methods, and the profession.
3. **Assurance/challenge authority** - inspect, challenge, or escalate within the function's mandate.

These do not automatically become operating authority. A business finance leader can report to the business president while maintaining a strong professional line to the corporate CFO. The corporate CFO may define financial standards, require qualified practice, and assure financial integrity without day-to-day command of the business finance team.

Practice leaders are conveners and stewards, not shadow executives. People belong simultaneously to their operating business, their profession, and Sable Harbor as an enterprise. This tri-belonging supports mobility without creating a matrix.

When a disagreement occurs, identify the decision class. Operating decisions sit with the operating owner; professional-standard decisions sit with the function; reserved enterprise matters escalate to the authority that owns them.

## Portfolio model

A named product or program does not automatically become a business. A Sable Harbor business earns autonomy when it has a coherent market or mission, its own economic engine, leadership capable of carrying it, and enough permanence that autonomy improves decisions.

The default hierarchy is flat: Sable Harbor -> business -> operating unit. Operating groups are not created merely to solve span-of-control aesthetics.

Business presidents run their businesses end to end inside their capital and authority envelopes. Corporate does not run the business through shadow functional executives. Shared capabilities exist where scale creates real leverage.

Capital is the primary connective tissue. Corporate need not command operations when it can allocate incremental capital, preserve enterprise standards, and hold business leaders accountable over time.

Acquired or emerging capability may be integrated into an existing business, operated as a distinct business, or held temporarily as an incubation/learning construct. Detailed rules for failed-business intervention and organic birth of new businesses are intentionally DEFERRED.

## Information and hierarchy

Hierarchy sets accountability, not epistemology. The person closest to a problem has an unfiltered right to contribute relevant facts without acquiring decision authority merely by proximity.

Corporate functions and J2 should reduce daisy-chain filtering. Material information can travel around management layers when necessary to preserve fidelity, subject to lawful information restrictions.
