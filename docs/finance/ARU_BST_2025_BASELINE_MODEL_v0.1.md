## Purpose

This document converts the ARU/BS&T headline case developed on September 5, 2026 into a bottom-up operating baseline. It does not invent a second business architecture. It implements the already-set structure:

- American Resource Utility is the acquired industrial-logistics operating company.
- Blood, Sweat & Tears Railway is ARU's wholly owned common-carrier shortline.
- ARU also operates industrial terminals/transload, heavy industrial trucking/drayage, and warehousing/materials handling.
- Red Wash is not part of the 2025 ARU baseline and generates no 2025 ARU revenue.
- Red Wash service is a separate post-acquisition intercompany opportunity.

The model is deliberately constrained to reconcile the accepted 2025 frame:

### Traffic concentration

- Soda ash, trona and related mineral traffic is the anchor franchise.
- Energy and oilfield materials provide a sticky but cyclical block.
- Aggregates/construction, industrial chemicals/metals, agriculture/ranch supply, and project/transload traffic keep the railroad from depending on one mine or commodity.
- Red Wash contributes **zero** to this 2025 schedule.
- The largest consolidated ARU customer is constrained to approximately 11% of revenue; the top five are approximately 40%.

### Interpretation

The model confirms the business conclusion already reached in the ARU work:

- BS&T is strategically important but financially thin.
- Most current EBITDA is generated by ARU's terminals, warehousing/material handling, and trucking operations.
- The non-rail segment margins—especially warehousing/material handling—are the highest-risk modeling assumption and require contract-level validation.
- The acquisition is not justified by extracting synergies from BS&T.
- Sable Harbor buys a functioning industrial platform with a railroad, then funds durability and selective expansion.

Red Wash integration capital is **not** included in the 2025 baseline. The current separate plan is:

- approximately $8.5M Phase 1;

- approximately $5.0M–$5.5M ARU strategic/multi-customer Taylor infrastructure;

- Taylor is the transload and last-mile hub;
- approximately 225 normalized recurring Red Wash rail loads, with a 300-load design allowance;
- intercompany revenue is eliminated in consolidation.

## 8. Supersession and implementation impact

This baseline supersedes the **economic scale** of the following reversible scenario assumptions for ARU/BS&T:

- `FIN-Q-005`: $23.6M ARU/BS&T external revenue.
- `FIN-Q-006`: $20.3M ARU/BS&T operating cost.

Those assumptions should not be changed in isolation. The current generation architecture books aggregate ARU economics to BS&T. The new baseline requires separate books or segment drivers for:

- BS&T;
- terminals/transload;
- trucking/drayage;
- warehousing/materials handling;
- ARU corporate/shared services;
- intercompany eliminations.

Until that implementation is complete, the old generated financial outputs remain technically reproducible but no longer represent the governing ARU business case.

## Model judgment requiring validation

The accepted $42.0M / $9.8M case is internally arithmetically coherent. Its main commercial risk is not BS&T's 9,000-carload schedule; it is whether the non-rail contract book supports approximately $8.3M of normalized EBITDA on $26.5M of revenue. That must be tested before purchase-price allocation and acquisition financing are treated as fully underwritten.
