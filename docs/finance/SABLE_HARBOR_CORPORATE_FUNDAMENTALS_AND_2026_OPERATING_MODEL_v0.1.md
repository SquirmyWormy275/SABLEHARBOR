## 18–20. Strategic tensions, factions, and unresolved risks

The 2026 company is held together by useful disagreement:

- Crossing veterans value practical resilience and exceptions; professionalizers require repeatability and explicit ownership.
- software economics reward standardization; mine and railway operations punish abstractions that ignore physical conditions;
- Foundry preserves competing meanings; management reporting still pressures teams to compress them;
- Willow prizes falsifiable experiments; operating leaders insist that experiments earn field access;
- Atlas needs commercialization evidence without overstating what investigation can establish;
- acquisitions create strategic proof but strain liquidity, integration capacity, safety governance, and management attention;
- Cradle’s smallest viable intervention conflicts with pressure to claim asset control;
- Advisory demand can pull scarce operators away from the work that made the method credible.

Major unresolved risks are Red Wash reserves and mine plan, permits and closure estimate, commodity-price exposure, ARU physical estate and labor terms, acquisition accounting, debt covenants, CoreCo churn/concentration, Atlas model/data rights, key-person knowledge, intercompany pricing, cyber/OT boundaries, environmental contingencies, contractor access, Emberline residual obligations, and the still-open federal boundary.
