# Records and decisions

[Wiki home](Home.md) · [Document library](Library.md) · [Locations](Locations.md) · [Open questions](Open-Questions.md)

Use this page to check where a company fact came from, what changed, and which record applies now. You do not need to follow the design history to read about a business or complete an accounting exercise.

## Choose the records you need

**For accounting or control testing,** start with the actual case records: agreements, invoices, ledgers, populations, procedures and results. The [practical exercises](../reader/exercises/README.md) and [transaction guide](../reader/transactions/README.md) identify those records and the period they cover. A decision about how the fictional company was written is not evidence that a transaction occurred or a control operated.

**For the company's design history,** use the sources below. Decision identifiers, approval history, superseded wording and publication checks belong here and in the underlying registers—not in every paragraph of a business description.

## How the decision history fits together

The [maintainer rules](../../MAINTAINERS.md) establish this order when records conflict:

1. Current accepted decisions, including the specific parts replaced by later decisions.
2. Board records within their approval scope.
3. Preserved design-session ledgers and originating records, unless superseded.
4. Company source documents.
5. Charts, publications, structured copies and search indexes derived from those sources.

Follow a fact from the current subject page to its source, then to the relevant decision and originating record. Follow a change in the other direction: the later decision identifies what it replaces; the earlier record remains available as history. The [detailed authority guide](../internal/CANON_AUTHORITY_AND_PUBLICATION_BOUNDARIES.md) explains the exceptions, source locks and publication rules. The [design-session ledger](../internal/CHAT_CANON_LEDGER_J2_ALEXANDRIA.md) preserves the originating J2 and Alexandria decisions.

Older workbooks and released documents keep the assumptions and sources used for their edition. A later company decision does not retrospectively change their results. Pull requests still awaiting acceptance are proposed changes, not a substitute for the accepted record.

## Locations

The [location directory](Locations.md) describes current accommodation and operations. These distinctions explain several misleading “location unknown” labels in older summaries:

| Subject | What is established | What that does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Pale Sun / Red Wash | Red Wash operates in the Great Divide Basin / Red Desert, north of Wamsutter in Sweetwater County, Wyoming. The industrial decisions establish the mine location. | A separate Pale Sun office address. Delaware is Pale Sun's incorporation jurisdiction, not its operating address. No accepted separate-office location was identified in this review. |
| Foundry / Foundry Field | Shared Reno and Sacramento accommodation, with customer-embedded and mobile field work, under the September 13 geographic decisions. | A separate Foundry campus or a completed fit-out of every proposed product floor. |
| Atlas Meridian | Shared Tucson and Sacramento accommodation under the same geographic decisions. | A separate Atlas-owned campus. |
| Willow / the Fort | Hazelwood in Pittsburgh; the accepted staged move took place in 2024. | The address of the earlier Klein shop, or a separate opening date for every shed. |
| Bedford | A selected fictional 15.27-acre redevelopment footprint in the Fairmont / White Hall area of West Virginia. | Real-world title, survey accuracy or an environmental clearance. |

The September 10 organization-chart edition predates these September 13 accommodation decisions. Its “Location not recorded” labels for Foundry Field and Atlas Meridian are not the current answer. Read the location directory alongside that dated chart edition. The chart artwork and older source snapshots remain preserved; their replacement requires the normal chart-publication process.

## For contributors

Write the company fact plainly in the article. Link to the relevant supporting record where it helps the reader, or to this page for the decision history. Keep material qualifications—such as a forecast rather than an actual result, an unperformed test, or a proposed rather than occupied building—next to the affected statement. Do not substitute repeated approval codes for an explanation.

A missing street address is not an unknown city. A shared office is not an unlocated business. Before carrying forward an unresolved label, check later decisions and distinguish the legal entity, operating site, office, historical premises and proposed facility.
