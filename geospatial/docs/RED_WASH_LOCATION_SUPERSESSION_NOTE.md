# Red Wash location supersession and Taylor rename

The user-approved governing addendum issued September 5, 2026 and accepted in the ARU/BS&T discussion on September 6 supersedes the earlier Carbon County map state. Its original text is preserved as `sources/GOVERNING_CANON_ADDENDUM_2026-09-05.md`.

|---|---|---|
| Red Wash regional location | Great Divide Basin / Red Desert, Sweetwater County, Wyoming, north of Wamsutter | Carbon County scenario |

| Railway | 38.81-mile legacy-scale proposal plus 4.23-mile later mine connection | No historical mine connection inferred; former point-to-point sketches do not govern |

Decisions GEO-D009 through GEO-D013 record the regional lock, anchor, Carbon supersession, town rename and working business scale. GEO-D014 records the analyst's preferred candidate, with proposal status. GEO-C001 is resolved; it is not an outstanding question.

Both original Red Wash PNG files retain their approved SHA-256 hashes. `assets/brand/red_wash_visual_manifest.json` now states that geographic labels are superseded, and the source-map folder has an explicit historical-use notice. Current maps use the approved anchor. The original Carbon point and the diagnostic UTM inversion remain in a historical layer, and the complete earlier review candidate is labeled and archived in `history/`.

Current-use geography was corrected in the Red Wash casebook, transaction/operating record, geological/technical report and core operating data. Earlier location values are preserved in a separate superseded scenario in the JSON. Old elevation, acreage, disturbance and geological scenario details have not been certified at the new anchor. A roughly 70 m DEM sample near the anchor is about 2,100 m elevation; it is not a surveyed portal height.

The approved artwork is not silently redrawn. New geospatial context and site-study sheets replace it for current geographic use. Future illustrative derivatives must use this corrected database and keep any conceptual subsurface layout distinct from surveyed/world-coordinate geometry.
