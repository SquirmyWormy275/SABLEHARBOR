**Version:** 0.2.0

**Scope:** Reconciled corporate lore versus inherited Sable Harbor canon and the Blackridge material currently available in the repository

---

## 1. Purpose

This audit tests the corporate-lore canon against the upstream Blackridge facts and the inherited architecture handover before the lore is treated as durable repository truth.

It is not an audit of financial plausibility, legal structure, mine economics, or the quantitative 2026 operating model. Those remain separate workstreams.

The test asks:

1. Does the new corporate history alter Blackridge’s locked physical, economic, or epistemic facts?
2. Are people inserted into Blackridge who were not established there?
3. Does the 2016–2026 history preserve the causal need for Foundry Field?
4. Are previously locked directions changed openly rather than silently?
5. Does the timeline contain impossible overlaps or products that exist before their prerequisites?
6. Are current-day business lines distinguished from discarded brainstorms and historical programs?

# 5. Explicit canon conflicts and resolutions

## 5.1 Emberline versus Evalon and Willow

### CONFLICT

The inherited handover treats Project Emberline as a current 2026 coal-focused internal venture and R&D program centered in Charleston.

The expanded lore creates:

- Evalon, Gid’s eastern coal/industrial experiment near Pittsburgh;
- Project Willow, the rechartered advanced-development laboratory;
- and a distinct role for Caleb’s coal field and commercial work.

### RESOLUTION — LOCKED SUPERSESSION

- Evalon is the Pittsburgh advanced-engineering precursor.
- Project Emberline is the formal Charleston-centered coal field/commercial venture created in 2022.
- Willow is created when Evalon is ended as a business concept and its experimental capability is preserved.
- Emberline continues through 2025 as paid coal R&D, field experimentation, and a qualification partner.
- Its enduring work is absorbed into Foundry, Willow, field operations, and emerging Advisory.
- Emberline is not a separate core 2026 division.

This retains the coal history and Caleb’s role while preventing Willow and Emberline from occupying the same institutional space.

## 5.2 Pale Sun incubation versus mine ownership

### CONFLICT

The inherited handover begins Pale Sun around 2026 as uranium, nuclear-supply-chain, federal-procurement, and possible segregated-environment incubation.

The expanded lore moves the thesis back into 2023–2024 and makes Red Wash ownership central by 2025.

### RESOLUTION — LOCKED SUPERSESSION WITH PRESERVED PRINCIPLE

Pale Sun becomes a uranium operating business centered on Red Wash.

The inherited control-first principle remains:

- uranium does not automatically create FedRAMP applicability;
- federal opportunities require boundary analysis;
- regulated information and systems are not casually commingled;
- and a separate federal or secure environment remains possible but not automatic.

The exact legal subsidiary and federal route remain open.

## 5.3 Foundry Field v0 in 2018 versus formal naming in 2021

### APPARENT CONFLICT

The handover says 2018 internal tools become Foundry Field v0. The voice-session lore says Foundry Field is named and standardized during 2020–2021.

### RESOLUTION — LOCKED

- 2018: internal reusable substrate begins as Foundry/retrospective Foundry v0.
- 2020–2021: Foundry Field becomes the formal remotely deployable commercial product identity.

The underlying technology can precede the final product name.

## 5.4 Atlas timeline

### APPARENT CONFLICT

The handover places the Atlas reboot in 2024, design partners in 2025, and controlled launch in 2026. The expanded lore describes a decisive 2026 naming and bridge-program moment.

### RESOLUTION — LOCKED

- “Atlas” is a thesis and research lineage beginning in 2022.
- It is seriously rebooted in 2024.
- Design-partner, Hound, and gauntlet work occur in 2025.
- Atlas Meridian becomes the formal product expression and enters a bridge/controlled-commercial phase in 2026.

The product is not conjured in 2026; the name and operating boundary crystallize then.

## 5.5 Mari Varela versus Ev/Evelyn placeholders

### RESOLUTION — LOCKED

All Ev, Evelyn, Ev Rourke, and Evelyn Teller Pale Sun-leader concepts are discarded.

The canonical Pale Sun operating leader is **Marianne “Mari” Varela**.

The earlier Teller/Enclave 51 Easter egg is not canon in this lore branch.

## 5.6 Operating model v0.1

### CONFLICT

The separate operating-model branch proposes, among other things:

- Emberline as a 2026 operating unit;
- Pale Sun as a small Federal Systems incubation team;
- a particular six-office/426-FTE structure;

The draft is not deleted and is not treated as wrong in every respect.

It must be revised in the next corporate-structure workstream to account for:

No quantitative value from that branch is locked by this lore push.

# 7. Plausibility dependencies not yet cleared

These are not narrative contradictions. They are validation tasks for later work.

## 7.1 Capital and company scale

Red Wash and ARU materially change the capital intensity, workforce, legal exposure, insurance, environmental obligations, and consolidated financial statements of Sable Harbor.

Before the operating model is locked, the company’s financing history must be rebuilt around:

- mine acquisition and restart capital;
- reclamation and closure obligations;
- ARU acquisition consideration;
- rolling stock, terminal, and infrastructure capital;
- working capital;
- commodity and logistics risk;
- and ring-fencing between software and operating assets.

## 7.2 Red Wash physical model

The mine still requires:

- geology and reserve model;
- mining method;
- ventilation and water assumptions;
- ore handling;
- mill and recovery flow sheet;
- yellowcake packaging and movement;
- workforce;
- permits;
- radiation protection;
- environmental history;
- closure plan;
- and restart schedule.

## 7.3 ARU logistics mechanism

The narrative establishes a custody and logistics boundary but intentionally does not state that all Red Wash uranium concentrate moves by rail.

The exact role of ARU and BS&T could involve:

- inbound reagents and consumables;
- equipment and bulk-material movements;
- truck-to-rail transload;
- terminals;
- regional mine-to-processing logistics;
- third-party customer traffic;
- and a controlled outbound transfer boundary.

This must be resolved against realistic uranium transport, security, and economics.

## 7.4 Sar-e-Sang governance

The incident is intentionally a governance gray zone.

Detailed canon must not accidentally state that Sable Harbor knowingly funded a designated terrorist organization, violated sanctions, or operated an intelligence mission unless the project deliberately chooses those consequences and models them fully.

The current canon is sufficient:

- loose authorization;
- itinerary drift;
- opaque intermediaries;
- untraceable cash;
- uncertain representations;
- and a leadership crisis.

## 7.5 Australia and Tasmania

The real locations used in the lore exist:

- Mole Creek Hotel, Mole Creek, Tasmania;
- Little Green Men Brewing Co. in the historic British Hotel, Deloraine, Tasmania.

South Australia is the principal Australian uranium-operating reference for the trip. Tasmania supplies archival, industrial-memory, and rare-earth context rather than a fictional claim that Tasmania hosts Australia’s major uranium mines.

# 8. Structural integrity tests

## 8.1 No universal-doom bias

### PASS

The timeline contains:

- scars: 2021 overcommitment, Sar-e-Sang, key-person dependence;
- neutral adaptation: Foundry configuration and local meaning;
- killed experiments: Glasshouse and Wallaby;
- techniques rather than products: Mule;
- successful innovation: Willow, Atlas Meridian, Cradle;
- ordinary maturity: Rachel Kim’s handoff, Foundry repeatability;
- and live operating constraints: Red Wash and ARU.

Not every event becomes a crisis.

## 8.2 No omniscient product

### PASS

Foundry cannot certify ground truth.

Atlas investigates rather than decides.

Willow experiments rather than governs production.

Pale Sun operations can stop a test.

Cradle must not break the host process.

ARU remains an operator with its own local intelligence.

## 8.3 No single villain

### PASS

The principal conflicts arise from legitimate roles:

- product coherence versus deployment reality;
- scientific precision versus customer urgency;
- experimental freedom versus operating accountability;
- mine ownership versus capital discipline;
- local knowledge versus formal representation;
- and platform reuse versus boundary integrity.

## 8.4 Blackridge remains separate

### PASS

Blackridge continues as:

- the founding wound;
- an independent forensic universe;
- a future Foundry and NAILEX benchmark;
- and a management/finance case.

The later company history does not consume or rewrite it.

# 9. Final audit conclusion

The corporate-lore canon is internally coherent enough to preserve in the repository.

It passes the available Blackridge cross-check because it:

- keeps the physical and economic failure intact;
- limits direct exposure to Daniel;
- positions the other founders and early employees after the event;
- treats the 2016–2018 incidents as recurrence tests rather than retcons;
- preserves The Crossing and COVID as the Foundry Field catalyst;
- creates Willow through an explicit Emberline/Evalon reconciliation;
- preserves the Atlas 2024–2026 arc;
- records Pale Sun as an intentional canon expansion;
- and leaves quantitative, legal, and physical implementation gaps open rather than disguising them.

The next required continuity audit should occur after either:

1. the complete detailed Blackridge canon is imported into the repository; or
2. the revised Corporate Fundamentals and 2026 Operating Model is drafted around Pale Sun, Cradle, ARU, Willow, and Advisory.
