# 4. FOUNDING STORY AND CORPORATE HISTORY

## 4.1 The founding wound: Blackridge

### LOCKED

Sable Harbor’s founders were connected through a 2015 mining failure involving:

**Argent Ridge Mining**
  
and its:

**Blackridge Mine**

Blackridge was a mid-sized open-pit copper-and-gold operation in Nevada.

The company was operating through years of accumulated system fragmentation:

- geology in one system;
- long-range and short-range planning in another;
- dispatch in another;
- maintenance in another;
- process-control data in another;
- laboratory results in another;
- finance and capital reporting elsewhere;
- critical reconciliations in spreadsheets and local databases.

Argent Ridge approved a major West Wall Phase 4 expansion after competent functional reviews. The problem was not a simple arithmetic error, fraud, or one reckless executive.

The organization assessed several risks independently:

- aging fleet;
- longer haul distances;
- delayed stripping;
- variable western ore;
- processing recovery;
- blend availability;
- capital schedule.

It did not represent their interaction as one coupled system.

The resulting causal chain was approximately:

1. fleet reliability weakened;
2. longer Phase 4 hauls consumed more effective capacity;
3. waste movement and stripping slipped;
4. exposed ore inventory declined;
5. blend flexibility narrowed;
6. the plant received a more difficult and variable feed mix;
7. recovery deteriorated;
8. mitigation consumed remaining operating flexibility;
9. the investment case fell below the company’s hurdle.

The governing finding is:

> Competence was real. Integration was absent.

A later reconstruction concluded that the relevant data had existed but could not be connected in time.

## 4.3 “The Crossing”

### LOCKED

**2020** is the existential period internally known as:

**The Crossing**

COVID-19 stops or sharply limits travel to mine sites. The services model collapses at precisely the point when the company is already financially vulnerable.

Sable Harbor has approximately twelve weeks of runway in the core narrative.

The company:

- freezes or reduces salaries;
- has founders take minimal compensation;
- confronts severe layoff scenarios;
- loses the ability to rely on on-site consultants;
- must make its integration technology remotely deployable.

The engineering challenge is not merely to build better analytics. It is to make onboarding, data extraction, mapping, transformation, and validation usable by mine customers whose primary jobs are mining, maintenance, metallurgy, finance, or operations—not data science.

Early attempts are inadequate:

- one version is powerful but too technical;
- another resembles spreadsheets with a thin wrapper;
- deployments still depend too heavily on Sable Harbor specialists.

The breakthrough is an automated and guided ETL process that asks customers what their systems and fields **mean**, rather than requiring them to design pipelines.

This produces the first genuinely remotely deployable Foundry Field.

The Crossing creates several lasting effects:

- product-market fit;
- strong customer loyalty;
- significant technical debt;
- founder mythology;
- an internal bias toward resilience and data correctness;
- a tendency for veterans to justify emergency shortcuts through Crossing-era experience;
- a widening cultural gap between early employees and later hires.

The Crossing should be inspiring but not sanitized. It saved the company while also planting later governance and architecture problems.

# 7. SHADOW IT AND PARALLEL REPRESENTATIONS OF WORK

## LOCKED AND CENTRAL

Shadow IT is a foundational Sable Harbor concept.

It must not be reduced to “employees use bad spreadsheets.”

Formal systems are often competent within their intended domains. The problem is that they do not answer every cross-functional question.

Shadow tools frequently arise because real work requires an answer that the formal system was never designed to produce.

Examples include:

- planner workbooks;
- maintenance bad-actor lists;
- personal production trackers;
- executive status sheets;
- Access databases;
- emailed CSV files;
- local SQL queries;
- macro-enabled spreadsheets;
- reconciliation files;
- bespoke crosswalks;
- unofficial dashboards;
- personal action lists.

Many are intelligent and useful. Some may locally outperform the official system.

They are also vulnerable to:

- unclear ownership;
- missing access control;
- manual transformations;
- stale mappings;
- undocumented logic;
- inconsistent versions;
- weak lineage;
- key-person dependency;
- silent changes;
- loss of context;
- unsupported executive compression.

## 7.1 Five shadow-artifact species

At minimum, model these categories:

1. **Gap-filling**
   - answers a question the official system does not answer.

2. **Translation**
   - converts one group’s language or status into another group’s terms.

3. **Roll-up**
   - compresses detailed work into management or executive reporting.

4. **Personal operational**
   - helps one person manage the actual state of their work.

5. **Reconciliation**
   - compares systems or competing representations and explains differences.

An artifact may belong to more than one category.

## 7.2 Trackers for trackers

Sable Harbor must represent the very common reality that one underlying project or work item may have several honest but conflicting status representations.

For example:

- the preparer records a workpaper as 80% complete;
- the manager’s tracker records it as not started because it has not reached the manager’s defined threshold;
- an executive report records the engagement as green because the due date remains achievable.

None of those necessarily involves deception.

They use different ontologies of “done.”

This phenomenon should be modeled as:

**parallel representations of work**

For every significant tracker or management artifact, preserve:

- subject population;
- business purpose;
- creator;
- owner;
- audience;
- definition of status;
- definition of completion;
- source systems;
- dependencies;
- transformations;
- cutoff time;
- refresh cadence;
- lineage;
- successor/predecessor versions;
- whether it is required;
- whether it is official;
- effective dates;
- known limitations.

## 7.3 Seam taxonomy

Sable Harbor should formally model:

- **identity seams:** one object has different identifiers across systems;
- **temporal seams:** event time, recording time, availability time, posting time, and decision time differ;
- **semantic seams:** the same word means different things to different functions;
- **representational seams:** different layers portray the same work differently;
- **provenance seams:** a conclusion or status has unclear origin or transformation history.

Foundry Field’s purpose is not to erase all local representations. It should preserve useful local intelligence while making relationships, meanings, transformations, and limitations visible.
