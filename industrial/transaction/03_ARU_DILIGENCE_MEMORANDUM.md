## Preserved investment disagreement

The buyer's financial reviewer argued that a 6.33× normalized EBITDA headline understated the $11M near-term capital burden and railroad renewal needs. The operating review agreed that the burden was real but argued it was finite and asset-specific, while the nonrail business retained customers and useful infrastructure. Neither side counted Red Wash as the answer. The board paper carries both views and the customer-loss/capital-overrun sensitivities.

Tessa objected to calling every owner-era cost an add-back. Only identified nonrecurring items survive the reported-to-normalized bridge. Nora objected to a broad centralization plan before the buyer understood local dispatch, working capital and customer interfaces. The adopted integration model protects those systems while improving evidence and succession.

## Gate disposition

Proceed to signing only with the $4.5M working-capital peg and exclusions explicitly defined, debt/lease treatment reconciled, final disclosure schedules, employment/retention instruments, claim/environmental protections, financing commitments and operating authority clear. Any newly discovered shutdown condition or material unpriced structural defect returns the acquisition to the board; none is silently authored into the base case.

The existing CBAs and relevant regulatory responsibilities are obligations, not points awarded in an evaluation. The participant may challenge the valuation, the durability of nonrail margins or the adequacy of reserves. This record preserves the buyer's contemporary rationale without prescribing a hidden correct conclusion.
