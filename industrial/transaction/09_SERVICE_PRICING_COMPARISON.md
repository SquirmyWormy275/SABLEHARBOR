## Reference editions and availability

Pacific Harbor Line's own [tariff directory](https://www.anacostia.com/resources/for-existing-customers/tariffs/phl/) links PHL 8000-U and PHL 8100-L. Both PDF covers say **issued January 30, 2026; effective January 1, 2026**. The earlier effective date does not establish January 1 publication or company knowledge. This record first observes them on September 5 Pacific time. Their published scope is PHL service in the Los Angeles/Long Beach harbor area, not the fictional Wyoming route.

The source editions are [PHL 8000-U, switching and miscellaneous charges](https://www.anacostia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026_Public-Tariff-PHL-8000-U.pdf) and [PHL 8100-L, storage and hazardous-material provisions](https://www.anacostia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026_PHL-8100-L-tariff.pdf). Page references below use printed PDF page numbers. They are carrier-published commercial terms, not universal legal requirements.

## Comparable units and material differences

| Item | Published carrier reference | Fictional service and inference |
|---|---|---|
| Rail switching | PHL 8000-U item 240, page 5: intra-terminal switching **$654.75 per car** | BS&T ordinary rail components are **$750–$950 per car**. They are $95.25–$295.25, or approximately 14.5%–45.1%, above this reference. The services differ in distance, density and infrastructure; this arithmetic does not establish a justified premium. |
| Dedicated exception capacity | PHL 8000-U item 140, page 3: first eight-hour two-person switching shift **$2,855.47**, including one locomotive/fuel, with **five shifts per week minimum** | Our included exception service and **$48,000 annual fixed allowance** are allocated support for a shared operation, not the purchase of a dedicated crew. They cannot be justified by converting a per-car rate into unlimited exclusive shifts. A sustained exception workload requires recosting. |
| Nonhazardous private-car storage | PHL 8100-L items 8200/8250, page 4: **$68.61 per car-day**, with two credit days and the tariff's calendar-day computation | Our contingent **$50 per car-day** after **72 elapsed hours**, or **96 for steel**, prorates hourly and requires customer causation. It is $18.61/day lower before differences in clocks and credits. No baseline chargeable dwell revenue is assumed. |
| Hazardous-material accessorial | PHL 8100-L item 8700, page 5: private hazardous/residue cars incur **$204.06 per car-day**, no credit days, commencing the day after interchange receipt | This is a separate exposure category. Our internal acid rate/free-time agreement does not waive a real connecting carrier's hazardous-material charges, storage restrictions or demurrage. It also creates no uranium custody permission. |

The first two reference rows are supported by [PHL 8000-U, items 140 and 240](https://www.anacostia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026_Public-Tariff-PHL-8000-U.pdf); the last two by [PHL 8100-L, items 8200, 8250, 8350 and 8700](https://www.anacostia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026_PHL-8100-L-tariff.pdf). These selected amounts are not a reproduction of the tariffs or a complete estimate of a PHL shipment.

## Cost-to-serve and decision

The [operations source](../source/operations.json) remains the case rate authority. Acid is **$950 rail + $700 terminal + four truck trips at $340 = $3,010 per car**. Binder is **$850 + $400 + four trips at $285 = $2,390**. Rail switching references cannot validate unloading equipment, chemical handling or truck cycle costs. The [rate and capital memorandum](../operations/RATE_AND_CAPITAL_MEMORANDUM.md) supplies those separately modeled resources and the 215-car normalized revenue base; its assumptions remain inspectable.

The comparison supports keeping rail, terminal, trucking, exceptional capacity and dwell as separate cost units. It does **not** substantiate the origin Class I linehaul price, chemical terminal price, Wyoming trucking rate, insurance premium or the whole $8.5M capital investment. No identical-route external quote has been obtained or fabricated. Procurement must compare a qualified alternative for the same payload, distance, frequency, equipment, exception coverage and liability allocation before treating the internal price as competitive. If the comparison fails, the agreement allows external service without a minimum-volume penalty.

Controller disposition: retain the current derived rates as the transparent case scenario, retain zero baseline demurrage, and carry external price confirmation as an explicit commercial condition. The $50 contingent rate and 72/96-hour free periods remain negotiated synthetic choices; neither is relabeled a general market norm merely because a carrier publishes a different schedule.
