## Commission and bounded curiosity

The September 11, 2023 commission asked why delivery confidence in a resource-customer dependency review was higher than the evidence supporting its downstream processing assumptions. Uranium was one dependency in that work. The commission did not ask J2 to build a uranium company.

On October 2, Vilander opened one bounded curiosity problem: **Where does reliable access to the next fuel-chain step matter more than nominal ownership of uranium?** Other assigned problems retained their owners. The curiosity allowance did not become permission for an unbounded acquisition program.

## Belief revisions

In October 2023 Vilander thought a cheap downstream asset might give Sable Harbor control of a bottleneck. By February 2024 he had learned that the capability, qualified customer relationship and liability boundary mattered more than the deed. By June he had stopped using a low acquisition price as evidence of low capital at risk. By October he had accepted that an expert's reputation could not substitute for claim-level verification.

The surviving thesis was narrower: a nuclear-materials platform could join operating knowledge, commercial rights and downstream intelligence while keeping each activity inside its licensed and technical competence. A credible first mine might teach the institution more than a nominally strategic asset it could not operate. This remained a human judgment, with serious unresolved downside.

## What did not fit

Historic pounds did not explain future cash. A permit list did not show a usable operating window. A consistent database could reproduce the same incorrect interpretation perfectly. A specialist could know the industry well and still be wrong about the proposed asset. These anomalies stayed in the Problem Book instead of being tidied out of the investment paper.

The October 11 recommendation was to develop Pale Sun and continue a bounded Red Wash case with Mari's hostile review and Martin Shaw's independent transaction discipline. It was not authority to buy a mine. Daniel and the enterprise's existing capital/governance process would decide whether to fund a company or acquire an asset.
