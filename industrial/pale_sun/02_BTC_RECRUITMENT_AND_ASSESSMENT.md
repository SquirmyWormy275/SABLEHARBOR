## Assessment packet: March 18, 2021

The panel presented a fictional contractor-control problem with a missed delivery, conflicting dispatch records, an intact receiving seal and a supervisor who insisted the carrier had arrived late. The candidate received the following deliberately incomplete materials; this packet is participant-visible institutional hiring history, not an evaluator key for the M&A case.

| Artifact | What it said | Evidentiary weakness Vilander identified |
|---|---|---|
| REC-A1, dispatch extract | Vehicle departed at 06:40 | System clock and later manual edits were not established |
| REC-A2, gate sheet | Receiving clerk wrote 09:25 | Handwriting did not establish whether entry was contemporaneous |
| REC-A3, supervisor note | Carrier delay caused the missed slot | Author had operational context and a commercial incentive |
| REC-A4, custody photograph | Seal number matched the shipping note | Identity evidence did not establish arrival time or responsibility |
| REC-A5, amended invoice | Overtime handling charge appeared after dispute | Charge could reflect real labor without proving cause |

Vilander refused to choose a culprit from those materials. He separated three questions: whether the load was the expected load, whether the delivery was late, and who bore the contractual consequence. He recommended preserving the load and original records, stopping a punitive charge until receipt facts were checked, obtaining clock offsets and speaking separately with dispatch and receiving. Essential operations could continue under a documented temporary arrangement.

The panel's March 19 discussion recorded a useful disagreement. One interviewer thought he had avoided the difficult decision; another noted that he had recommended an immediate reversible action and specified the evidence needed for an irreversible one. Vilander's read-back identified what would change his recommendation: a reliable independent arrival record, a documented contractual cutoff, or physical evidence requiring a safety hold. He also acknowledged that perfect timing reconstruction might remain impossible.

Compensation exceeded his expected BTC succession earnings; the exact amount is not a case input. It was not a quota for reports, a promise of promotion or a gamified accuracy score. His first two years required repetitions and institutional trust.
