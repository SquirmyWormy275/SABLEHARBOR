# 1. Regulatory architecture

Red Wash is modeled as a conventional underground uranium mine with a compact conventional mill and tailings system. The controlling regulatory architecture includes:

- Bureau of Land Management surface management under 43 CFR Subpart 3809 where federal lands and mining claims are involved;
- National Environmental Policy Act review for the federal Plan of Operations;
- Wyoming Department of Environmental Quality non-coal mine permitting and reclamation;
- source-material licensing under Wyoming Agreement State authority, consistent with 10 CFR Parts 20 and 40 and Part 40 Appendix A;
- U.S. EPA uranium/thorium mill-tailings standards under 40 CFR Part 192;
- radon-emission requirements under 40 CFR Part 61 Subpart W;
- Wyoming air, groundwater, surface-water, and stormwater programs;
- Mine Safety and Health Administration metal/nonmetal requirements;
- ATF explosives controls;
- DOT/PHMSA hazardous-material transport requirements;
- Wyoming State Engineer water-right administration.

Wyoming assumed regulatory authority over uranium milling in 2018. The fictional source-material license is therefore modeled as an NRC-origin license transferred to Wyoming.

# 6. Air, water, groundwater, and tailings

## 6.1 Air and radon

The synthetic air authorization covers crushing, grinding, drying, engines, process vents, haul roads, stockpiles, and fugitive dust. The radon program models operational tailings under Subpart W and includes monitoring, work-practice, record, inspection, and corrective-action obligations.

## 6.2 Stormwater and drainage

The WYPDES program defines industrial drainage, outfalls, best management practices, inspections, sampling, spills, maintenance, and corrective action. The site map identifies process water separately from stormwater.

## 6.3 Groundwater

The groundwater program defines background, points of compliance, wells, parameters, frequency, laboratory and field QA/QC, statistical evaluation, action levels, notification, assessment, corrective action, and reporting.

The synthetic 2019–2026 series includes MW-04, MW-09, MW-12, MW-17, and MW-21. MW-17 develops an upward uranium and sulfate trend after 2023. The public record treats this as material evidence requiring integrated investigation. It does not state that one trend proves a completed release pathway or final liability.

## 6.4 Tailings

Tailings controls include liner and underdrain systems, freeboard, water balance, beach management, inspection, seepage/underdrain response, radon control, dust, phased use, reclamation, and final cover. Cell 1 underdrain records and MW-17 groundwater records were found in separate data-room areas and became more consequential when reconciled.

# 8. MSHA, occupational safety, and emergency response

The Red Wash safety program applies the federal metal/nonmetal framework to underground and surface activities. It includes:

- workplace examinations;
- new-miner, experienced-miner, annual refresher, task, and contractor training;
- ground-control plan and inspections;
- ventilation and air-quality management;
- escapeways, refuge, communications, and evacuation;
- mobile equipment and traffic controls;
- electrical safety and lockout/tagout;
- explosives storage and use;
- hoisting and lifting;
- chemical, acid, and oxidant handling;
- confined space;
- respiratory protection and industrial hygiene;
- incident, injury, illness, and near-miss reporting;
- emergency medical and mine-rescue coordination;
- corrective-action verification.

Radiation protection supplements rather than replaces mine-safety requirements.

Historical events include one lost-time hand injury during commissioning, several near misses, a ventilation interruption, a loader fire, a minor acid splash, the September 2025 combined-condition stop, and a later OT communication loss. The case preserves ordinary operational learning without manufacturing a disaster sequence.

# 11. Closure, reclamation, ARO, and surety

The current-cost closure basis is $25.0 million. Scope includes:

- progressive reclamation during operations;
- underground sealing and demobilization;
- decontamination and demolition of mill and associated facilities;
- management/disposition of contaminated equipment and materials;
- final tailings cover and radon barrier;
- process-water and groundwater management;
- earthwork, drainage, topsoil, and revegetation;
- environmental monitoring and corrective work;
- long-term surveillance and transfer.

The accounting ARO is $16.0 million using a 2.5% inflation assumption, 6.5% discount assumption, and staged cash flows through 2039. This is not the regulatory bond. Sable Harbor annually reconciles engineering current cost, accounting present value, surety, actual work completed, changed conditions, and permit obligations.
